# sourced
1 | At Gibeon God offers Solomon whatever he asks, and Solomon asks for wisdom and knowledge to lead the people. || Chronicles explains Solomon’s journey to Gibeon in more detail than 1 Kings 3:4, as a public event tied to his becoming king [[tosn:2 Chronicles 1:2–6]].
2 | Solomon arranges with Huram of Tyre for materials and skilled workers to build the temple. || Huram-abi, son of a woman of Dan [[bible:2 Chronicles 2:14]], was a master craftsman like Bezalel, who built the tabernacle [[tosn:2 Chronicles 2:13–14]] [[bible:Exodus 31:1-5]].
3 | Solomon builds the temple on Mount Moriah, where God appeared to David. || Chronicles stresses the temple’s location on Mount Moriah, where the plague ended at the threshing floor; Abraham bound Isaac in the land of Moriah, and tradition links the two places [[tosn:2 Chronicles 3:1]].
4 | The bronze altar, the great Sea, the lampstands and the temple furnishings are made. || Chronicles gives less detail on the temple’s furnishings than Kings does [[tosn:2 Chronicles 3:1–4]] [[bible:1 Kings 7:13-51]].
5 | The ark is brought in; as the singers praise God, “for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever”, the cloud of glory fills the temple. || The Chronicler lists the singers and 120 trumpeters at the dedication [[tosn:2 Chronicles 5:11–14]]. As they praised God “for he is good”, the cloud filled the temple [[bible:2 Chronicles 5:13-14]].
6 | Solomon blesses the people and prays at the dedication of the temple. || Solomon’s prayer ends by quoting Psalm 132:8–10 [[tosn:2 Chronicles 6:41–42]].
7 | Fire falls from heaven. God answers: “If my people… humble themselves, pray, seek my face, and turn… then I will hear from heaven, will forgive their sin, and will heal their land.” || God promises to hear “if my people… will humble themselves, pray, seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways” [[bible:2 Chronicles 7:14]]. Manasseh’s later repentance meets its terms [[tosn:2 Chronicles 33:12]].
8 | Solomon’s building projects and his ordering of worship. || Solomon housed Pharaoh’s daughter away from David’s palace, because the places where the ark had come were holy [[bible:2 Chronicles 8:11]] [[tosn:2 Chronicles 8:11]].
9 | The Queen of Sheba visits; Solomon’s wealth and death. || Sheba, roughly modern Yemen, was rich from trading frankincense and myrrh [[tosn:2 Chronicles 9:1–2]]. Chronicles ends Solomon’s story without the account of his foreign wives and idols in 1 Kings 11 [[bible:1 Kings 11:1-13]] [[bible:2 Chronicles 9:29-31]].
10 | Rehoboam refuses to lighten the people’s burden, and the northern tribes break away. || The account follows 1 Kings 12 [[bible:1 Kings 12:1-24]].
11 | Rehoboam strengthens Judah; priests and faithful people from the north move south. || Priests and Levites left the north for Judah because Jeroboam would not let them serve God [[bsn:2 Chronicles 10:1–12:16]] [[tosn:2 Chronicles 11:5–23]].
12 | Rehoboam abandons God’s law, and Pharaoh Shishak invades. He humbles himself and is not destroyed. || Shishak (Shoshenq I) ruled Egypt from 945 to 924 BC [[tosn:2 Chronicles 12:2–4]]. When the leaders humbled themselves, God spared them from destruction [[bible:2 Chronicles 12:7]].
13 | Abijah of Judah defeats Jeroboam of Israel after a speech about God’s covenant with David. || Abijah is called Abijam in Kings [[tosn:2 Chronicles 13:1]], and Chronicles adds a story of him not found in Kings [[bsn:2 Chronicles 10:1–12:16]]. “God is with us at our head” [[bible:2 Chronicles 13:12]].
14 | Asa removes foreign altars and defeats a huge Ethiopian army after praying. || Asa ruled from 910 to 869 BC [[tosn:2 Chronicles 14:1]]. He prays, “Help us, Yahweh our God; for we rely on you” [[bible:2 Chronicles 14:11]].
15 | The prophet Azariah urges Asa to seek God; Asa leads a covenant renewal. || The prophet Azariah’s message was also the Chronicler’s message to Judah after the exile: the LORD stays with those who stay with him [[tosn:2 Chronicles 15:1–7]] [[bible:2 Chronicles 15:2]].
16 | Late in life Asa relies on Aram instead of God, imprisons a prophet, and seeks doctors rather than God when ill. || Late in life Asa stopped trusting God and imprisoned the prophet Hanani [[bsn:2 Chronicles 14:2–16:14]] [[tosn:2 Chronicles 16:7–9]]. “Yahweh’s eyes run back and forth throughout the whole earth” [[bible:2 Chronicles 16:9]].
17 | Jehoshaphat strengthens Judah and sends officials and Levites to teach the law in every town. || Jehoshaphat obeyed the command to teach the law, sending officers, Levites and priests through Judah [[tosn:2 Chronicles 17:7–9]].
18 | Jehoshaphat allies with Ahab, and Micaiah alone foretells disaster. Ahab dies. || The account follows 1 Kings 22 [[bible:1 Kings 22:1-40]].
19 | A prophet rebukes Jehoshaphat’s alliance, and he appoints judges, telling them to judge without bribes. || Jehoshaphat set judges in the towns and a central court in Jerusalem, urging integrity [[tosn:2 Chronicles 19:4–11]]: “Deal courageously, and may Yahweh be with the good” [[bible:2 Chronicles 19:11]].
20 | Facing invasion, Jehoshaphat prays, “We don’t know what to do, but our eyes are on you.” Singers lead the army, and the enemies destroy each other. || Jehoshaphat prays in line with Solomon’s prayer [[tosn:2 Chronicles 20:3–12]]. When the singers began to praise, the LORD struck the enemy [[bible:2 Chronicles 20:21-22]].
21 | Jehoram of Judah follows Ahab’s ways and dies of a painful disease, unmourned. || The prophet Elijah wrote Jehoram a letter warning of punishment [[tosn:2 Chronicles 21:12–15]] [[bible:2 Chronicles 21:12]].
22 | Ahaziah reigns briefly and is killed; Athaliah seizes power. Baby Joash is hidden. || Athaliah, like her mother Jezebel, tried to wipe out the royal heirs [[tosn:2 Chronicles 22:2–4]] [[tosn:2 Chronicles 22:10–12]], but Joash was hidden [[bible:2 Chronicles 22:11]].
23 | The priest Jehoiada crowns Joash and Athaliah is killed. The people make a covenant to be God’s people. || The account follows 2 Kings 11 [[bible:2 Kings 11:1-21]].
24 | Joash repairs the temple, but after Jehoiada dies he turns to idols and has Jehoiada’s son Zechariah stoned. || Zechariah, son of the priest who had saved Joash, was murdered in the temple court by Joash himself; Jesus refers to this murder [[tosn:2 Chronicles 24:20–22]] [[bible:Luke 11:51]].
25 | Amaziah serves God half-heartedly, defeats Edom, then worships Edom’s gods. || Amaziah did right, “but not with a perfect heart” [[bible:2 Chronicles 25:2]] [[tosn:2 Chronicles 25:2]].
26 | Uzziah becomes powerful but proudly burns incense in the temple and is struck with a skin disease. || Uzziah’s leprosy was punishment for burning incense in the temple, which only priests could do [[tosn:2 Chronicles 26:16–18]]. Isaiah’s vision came “in the year that King Uzziah died” [[bible:Isaiah 6:1]].
27 | Jotham does right and becomes mighty. || Jotham followed God with all his heart [[bsn:2 Chronicles 25:1–28:27]].
28 | Ahaz worships idols, sacrifices his sons, and shuts the temple. Israel takes captives but returns them on a prophet’s word. || Ahaz led the people into idolatry and sacrificed children [[bsn:2 Chronicles 25:1–28:27]]. The northern men clothed, fed and returned their captives [[bible:2 Chronicles 28:15]].
29 | Hezekiah reopens and purifies the temple, and worship is restored with great joy. || Hezekiah reigned from 715 to 686 BC [[tosn:2 Chronicles 29:1]]. His first act was to repair the temple doors, a pun on his name, “Yah makes strong” [[tosn:2 Chronicles 29:3–4]].
30 | Hezekiah invites all Israel, north and south, to a great Passover. Many come, and God hears their prayer. || The Passover story shows the Chronicler’s concern for the unity of all Israel [[tosn:2 Chronicles 30:1–27]]. Holding it a month late followed the spirit of Numbers 9 [[tosn:2 Chronicles 30:2–3]].
31 | Idols are destroyed, and the people bring tithes in abundance. || Hezekiah “did it with all his heart, and prospered” [[bible:2 Chronicles 31:21]].
32 | Sennacherib invades; Hezekiah trusts God and Jerusalem is saved. Hezekiah’s pride, then humility. || The parallel accounts are 2 Kings 18–20 and Isaiah 36–39 [[bible:2 Kings 18:13-37]] [[bible:Isaiah 36:1-22]].
33 | Manasseh leads Judah into great evil, is taken to Babylon in chains, repents, and is restored. Amon follows. || Manasseh reigned 55 years, longer than any other king of Judah [[tosn:2 Chronicles 33:1–20]]. His humbling met the terms of 7:14 [[tosn:2 Chronicles 33:12]]. The later Prayer of Manasseh, probably written in Greek between 250 BC and AD 50, is based on this [[tbd:016:Prayer of Manasseh]].
34 | Josiah seeks God as a youth, purges idols, repairs the temple, and the Book of the Law is found. || The account follows 2 Kings 22–23 [[bible:2 Kings 22:1-20]].
35 | Josiah keeps a great Passover, then dies fighting Pharaoh Neco. Jeremiah laments him. || There was no Passover like it “from the days of Samuel the prophet” [[bible:2 Chronicles 35:18]]. Josiah would not listen to “the words of Neco from the mouth of God” [[bible:2 Chronicles 35:22]].
36 | The last kings, the burning of the temple and exile to Babylon “to fulfill Yahweh’s word by Jeremiah’s mouth”. Cyrus of Persia decrees that the exiles may return to build the temple. || Jeremiah foretold the length of the exile, and the land’s Sabbath rest fulfils Leviticus 26 [[tosn:2 Chronicles 36:21]]. The closing verses introduce Ezra [[tosn:2 Chronicles 36:22–23]], and in the traditional Jewish order Chronicles is the last book of the Hebrew Bible [[tbd:026:Zechariah, Book of]].
