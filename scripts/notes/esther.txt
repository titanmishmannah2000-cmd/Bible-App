# sourced
1 | At a lavish feast King Ahasuerus (Xerxes) of Persia orders Queen Vashti to display her beauty; she refuses and is deposed. || Ahasuerus is Xerxes, whose father Darius I ruled from 521 to 486 BC [[tosn:Esther 1:1]]; Xerxes ruled from 486 to 465 BC [[tbd:002:Book of Ezra]]. His empire ran from India to Ethiopia [[tosn:Esther 1:1]].
2 | Esther, a Jewish orphan raised by her cousin Mordecai, is chosen as queen, keeping her identity secret. Mordecai uncovers a plot against the king. || Hadassah is a Hebrew name meaning “myrtle”; Esther is a Persian name meaning “star” [[tosn:Esther 2:7]].
3 | Haman, the king’s top official, is enraged when Mordecai will not bow to him, and plots to destroy all the Jews. || Haman is called an Agagite; Agag was an Amalekite king and Saul’s enemy [[tbd:001:Agagite]] [[bible:1 Samuel 15:8]].
4 | Mordecai urges Esther to plead with the king, though going uninvited could mean death: “Who knows if you haven’t come to the kingdom for such a time as this?” || Their fasting and mourning suggest they were praying for God to act, though his name is not mentioned [[tosn:Esther 4:3]]. Mordecai trusted God’s promise not to let his people be destroyed [[tosn:Esther 4:13–14]].
5 | Esther invites the king and Haman to a banquet. Haman builds a gallows for Mordecai. || It was customary not to seem eager when asking a favour, and the delay gave the king time to learn of Mordecai’s loyalty [[tosn:Esther 5:8]].
6 | Unable to sleep, the king learns of Mordecai’s good deed, and Haman is forced to honour him publicly. || The king’s sleepless night shows God protecting Esther and Mordecai [[tosn:Esther 6:1]].
7 | At the second banquet Esther reveals Haman’s plot, and Haman is hanged on the gallows he built for Mordecai. || Haman dying on the pole he built for Mordecai is a great reversal that shows God’s guiding hand [[tosn:Esther 7:9]].
8 | Mordecai is promoted, and a new decree allows the Jews to defend themselves. || Persian law could not be revoked once written, but a new decree could give competing orders [[tosn:Esther 8:8]].
9 | The Jews defeat their attackers, and the feast of Purim is established. || Purim means “lots” [[tbd:016:Purim]], after the lot Haman cast [[bible:Esther 3:7]]. The festival celebrates God giving his people rest from their enemies [[tosn:Esther 9:19]].
10 | Mordecai becomes second only to the king and seeks the good of his people. || The Greek Old Testament adds extra passages to Esther, including prayers of Mordecai and Esther [[tbd:005:Esther, Additions to]]; these are part of the Apocrypha in English Bibles [[tbd:002:Book of Esther]].
