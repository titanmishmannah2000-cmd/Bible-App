1 | Paul thanks God for the Corinthians but rebukes their divisions: “I follow Paul,” “I follow Apollos.” The message of the cross is foolishness to the world, but God’s power. || “We preach Christ crucified… Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God.”
2 | Paul came not with clever speech but in weakness, so faith would rest on God’s power. The Spirit reveals God’s wisdom. || “Things which an eye didn’t see… these God has prepared for those who love him.”
3 | The church is God’s field and God’s building; Paul planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. “You are a temple of God.” || Christ is the only foundation; each builder’s work will be tested by fire.
4 | Apostles are servants and stewards; Paul describes their hardships and calls the Corinthians to imitate him. || Paul uses irony to deflate their pride.
5 | A man living with his father’s wife must be removed from the church. A little yeast leavens the whole lump. || “Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed in our place.”
6 | Believers should not take each other to court. Sexual immorality is a sin against the body: “Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit.” “You were bought with a price.” || The body matters to God.
7 | Paul answers questions on marriage, singleness and divorce, valuing both marriage and singleness as gifts. || Paul distinguishes his own advice from “the Lord’s” command.
8 | On food offered to idols: knowledge puffs up, but love builds up. Don’t let your freedom make a weaker believer stumble. || “There is one God, the Father… and one Lord, Jesus Christ” (8:6), an early confession.
9 | Paul gives up his rights for the sake of the gospel: “I have become all things to all men, that I may by all means save some.” || The athlete’s discipline pictures the Christian life.
10 | Israel’s wilderness story is a warning. “God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted above what you are able.” Do everything for God’s glory. || Paul calls the rock in the wilderness Christ.
11 | Head coverings in worship, and abuses at the Lord’s Supper. Paul repeats the tradition: “This is my body… this cup is the new covenant in my blood.” || The earliest written account of the Last Supper, about AD 54.
12 | Spiritual gifts: one Spirit, many gifts. The church is one body with many parts; each needs the others. || The body metaphor values the weak and the honoured alike.
13 | “If I speak with the languages of men and of angels, but don’t have love…” “Love is patient and is kind… love never fails.” “Now faith, hope, and love remain… The greatest of these is love.” || The famous hymn of love (agapē), written to a church proud of its gifts.
14 | Prophecy builds up the church more than tongues. Worship should be orderly and understandable. || “God is not a God of confusion, but of peace.”
15 | Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to Peter, the Twelve, five hundred, James and Paul. If Christ is not raised, faith is in vain. The resurrection body. “Death, where is your sting?” || 15:3–7 is a creed Paul received, probably within a few years of the crucifixion: one of the earliest witnesses to the resurrection.
16 | A collection for the poor in Jerusalem, travel plans and greetings. “Marana tha”: “Come, Lord!” || The Aramaic prayer shows the earliest believers prayed to Jesus as Lord.
