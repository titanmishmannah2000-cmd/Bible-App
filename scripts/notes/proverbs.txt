# sourced
1 | The purpose of Proverbs: to give wisdom to the young. “The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of knowledge.” A father warns his son against violent gangs, and Wisdom calls in the streets. || Solomon is the authority on Israel’s wisdom, but he did not write every proverb [[tosn:Proverbs 1:1]]. The fear of the Lord is stressed throughout the book [[tbd:016:Proverbs, Book of]] [[bible:Proverbs 1:7]].
2 | If you search for wisdom like hidden treasure, you will understand the fear of God; wisdom will protect you from evil men and the seductive woman. || The father urges his son to seek wisdom, knowing it is a gift from God [[tosn:Proverbs 2:1–22]], searched for like hidden treasure [[tosn:Proverbs 2:4]].
3 | “Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don’t lean on your own understanding.” Honour God with your wealth; don’t despise his discipline. || The Lord blesses those who trust him, and his correction helps them [[tosn:Proverbs 3:1–12]]. Hebrews 12 quotes 3:11–12 on God’s discipline [[tosn:Proverbs 3:11–12]] [[bible:Hebrews 12:5-6]].
4 | A father passes on his own father’s teaching: get wisdom; guard your heart, for out of it flow the springs of life. || The heart is central in the struggle between wisdom and folly [[tosn:Proverbs 4:23]]. “The path of the righteous is like the dawning light” [[bible:Proverbs 4:18]].
5 | A warning against adultery, and a call to rejoice in the wife of your youth. || Following instruction helps in good choices, especially with an immoral woman [[tosn:Proverbs 5:1–2]]; the husband is told to rejoice in the wife of his youth [[bible:Proverbs 5:18]].
6 | Warnings against rash pledges and laziness (“Go to the ant, you sluggard”), and seven things God hates. || Securing someone else’s debt means pledging your own goods [[tosn:Proverbs 6:1–5]]. “Go to the ant, you sluggard” [[bible:Proverbs 6:6]].
7 | A vivid story of a naive young man led astray by an adulterous woman at twilight. || The last of the warnings in chapters 1–9 about immoral women [[tosn:Proverbs 7:1–27]].
8 | Lady Wisdom speaks: she was with God before creation, “the craftsman by his side” rejoicing in his world, and whoever finds her finds life. || One of the most remarkable personifications in the Old Testament [[tbd:016:Proverbs, Book of]]. Wisdom calls publicly, like a prophet [[tosn:Proverbs 8:1–2]], and Christians see Jesus as the high point of God’s wisdom [[tosn:Proverbs 8:22–25]] [[bible:1 Corinthians 1:24]].
9 | Wisdom and Folly each prepare a feast and invite the simple; one leads to life, the other to death. || Wisdom and Folly each offer a meal [[tosn:Proverbs 9:1–6]]; Folly stands for worldly wisdom [[tosn:Proverbs 8:1–9:18]]. “The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of wisdom” [[bible:Proverbs 9:10]].
10 | The proverbs of Solomon begin: two-line sayings contrasting the wise and foolish, the diligent and lazy, the righteous and wicked. || The Proverbs of Solomon begin: short sayings, often in contrasting pairs [[tosn:Proverbs 10:1–22:16]] [[tbd:016:Proverbs, Book of]]. 1 Peter 4:8 echoes “love covers all wrongs” [[bible:Proverbs 10:12]] [[bible:1 Peter 4:8]].
11 | Honest weights please God; pride brings disgrace; the generous will prosper. || Generous people succeed, but hoarders lose [[tosn:Proverbs 11:24–26]]. “He who is wise wins souls” [[bible:Proverbs 11:30]].
12 | Whoever loves discipline loves knowledge; a wise person listens to advice; careless words pierce like a sword. || “The tongue of the wise heals” [[bible:Proverbs 12:18]].
13 | Hope deferred makes the heart sick; walk with the wise and become wise; one who spares the rod hates his son. || Controlling your words shows wisdom [[tosn:Proverbs 13:3]] [[bible:James 3:2]].
14 | “There is a way which seems right to a man, but in the end it leads to death.” Even in laughter the heart may be sorrowful. || “There is a way which seems right to a man, but in the end it leads to death” [[bible:Proverbs 14:12]]. Wise people help the poor [[tosn:Proverbs 14:31]].
15 | “A gentle answer turns away wrath.” Better a little with the fear of God than great treasure with trouble. || Managing anger shows wisdom [[tosn:Proverbs 15:18]] [[bible:James 1:19-20]].
16 | “Commit your deeds to Yahweh, and your plans shall succeed.” Pride goes before destruction. People plan, but God directs their steps. || Our plans succeed only if God permits [[tosn:Proverbs 16:1]]. Even the lot’s “every decision is from Yahweh” [[bible:Proverbs 16:33]].
17 | Better a dry crust with peace than a feast with strife. A friend loves at all times. A cheerful heart is good medicine. || “A cheerful heart makes good medicine” [[bible:Proverbs 17:22]]. “A friend loves at all times” [[bible:Proverbs 17:17]].
18 | The name of Yahweh is a strong tower. There is a friend who sticks closer than a brother. || Words reflect character, like water from a spring [[tosn:Proverbs 18:4]]. “Death and life are in the power of the tongue” [[bible:Proverbs 18:21]].
19 | Whoever is kind to the poor lends to God. Many plans are in a person’s heart, but God’s purpose will stand. || “He who has pity on the poor lends to Yahweh; he will reward him” [[bible:Proverbs 19:17]].
20 | Wine is a mocker; love not sleep; the human spirit is the lamp of God searching the inner self. || Pride stops people from seeing their faults [[tosn:Proverbs 20:9]]. Do not take revenge, but wait for the LORD [[tosn:Proverbs 20:22]] [[bible:Romans 12:17-19]].
21 | The king’s heart is in God’s hand like streams of water. To do justice is more acceptable to God than sacrifice. || “To do righteousness and justice is more acceptable to Yahweh than sacrifice” [[bible:Proverbs 21:3]], as the prophets also say [[bible:Hosea 6:6]].
22 | A good name is better than great riches. “Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it.” || From 22:17 comes a section of “the wise”, with thirty sayings [[tosn:Proverbs 22:17–24:22]]. 22:17–23:11 closely parallels the Egyptian Instruction of Amenemope; most think it adapts an Egyptian original, though some Egyptologists argue the reverse [[tbd:016:Proverbs, Book of]].
23 | Sayings of the wise: on table manners with rulers, not wearing yourself out to get rich, and the dangers of wine. || Many sayings advise young men serving rulers, such as resisting a ruler’s rich food [[tosn:Proverbs 23:1–3]]. The drunkard is described in 23:29–35 [[bible:Proverbs 23:29-35]].
24 | Don’t envy evil people. Rescue those being led to death. A little sleep, a little slumber, and poverty comes. || “Don’t rejoice when your enemy falls” introduces Jesus’s teaching to love enemies [[tosn:Proverbs 24:17–18]] [[bible:Matthew 5:44]].
25 | More proverbs copied by King Hezekiah’s men. “A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in settings of silver.” If your enemy is hungry, give him food. || King Hezekiah’s advisers, in his reign of 728–686 BC, gathered these proverbs of Solomon [[tosn:Proverbs 25:1–29:27]]. Paul quotes 25:21–22 [[tosn:Proverbs 25:21–22]] [[bible:Romans 12:20]].
26 | Fools, sluggards and gossips are described with sharp humour: “As a dog that returns to his vomit…” || Proverbs often depend on the situation, as 26:4–5 shows side by side [[tosn:Proverbs 26:4–5]]. 2 Peter 2:22 quotes 26:11 [[tosn:Proverbs 26:11]].
27 | Don’t boast about tomorrow. Faithful are the wounds of a friend. Iron sharpens iron. || Plan, but plans succeed only if God wills [[tosn:Proverbs 27:1]] [[bible:James 4:13-15]]. “Iron sharpens iron” [[bible:Proverbs 27:17]].
28 | The wicked flee when no one pursues, but the righteous are bold as a lion. Whoever conceals sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses finds mercy. || “The righteous are as bold as a lion” [[bible:Proverbs 28:1]]. “Whoever confesses and renounces them finds mercy” [[bible:Proverbs 28:13]].
29 | Where there is no revelation, the people cast off restraint. The fear of man is a snare, but whoever trusts in God is safe. || The WEB reads 29:18, “Where there is no revelation, the people cast off restraint” [[bible:Proverbs 29:18]]. “The fear of man proves to be a snare” [[bible:Proverbs 29:25]] [[tosn:Proverbs 29:25]].
30 | The sayings of Agur: humble wonder at God, prayer for neither poverty nor riches, and numerical sayings about wonderful and small things. || Nothing is known of Agur; he probably came from Massa in north Arabia, a region known for wisdom [[tbd:016:Proverbs, Book of]]. Admitting ignorance is the first step to wisdom [[tosn:Proverbs 30:1–3]]. “Every word of God is flawless” [[bible:Proverbs 30:5]].
31 | King Lemuel’s mother teaches him to defend the poor, then a poem praises the woman of strength: hard-working, wise, generous and fearing God. || Lemuel was probably also from Massa [[tosn:Proverbs 31:1–9]]. The closing poem is an acrostic that feels complete and helps learners [[tosn:Proverbs 31:10–31]], and the worthy woman, like wisdom, is worth more than rubies [[tosn:Proverbs 31:10–12]].
