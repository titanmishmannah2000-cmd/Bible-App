1 | “Blessed be… God of all comfort; who comforts us in all our affliction, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction.” Paul explains his change of plans. || All God’s promises are “Yes” in Christ.
2 | Paul wrote a painful letter out of love; now the offender should be forgiven and comforted. We are the aroma of Christ. || A tearful letter mentioned here seems to have been lost, or may be part of chapters 10–13.
3 | The Corinthians are a letter from Christ written by the Spirit. The new covenant is more glorious than Moses’s; “where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.” || Paul reflects on the veil over Moses’s face (Exodus 34).
4 | “We have this treasure in clay vessels.” “Pressed on every side, yet not crushed.” “The things which are seen are temporal, but the things which are not seen are eternal.” || A classic text on suffering and hope.
5 | Our earthly tent will be replaced by a heavenly dwelling. “If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation.” God gave us the ministry of reconciliation: “Be reconciled to God.” || 5:21: “Him who knew no sin he made to be sin on our behalf.”
6 | “Now is the acceptable time… now is the day of salvation.” Paul lists his hardships. “Don’t be unequally yoked with unbelievers.” || Paul quotes Isaiah 49:8.
7 | Paul rejoices at Titus’s news that the Corinthians repented. “godly sorrow produces repentance to salvation.” || The distinction between worldly sorrow and godly sorrow.
8 | The Macedonians gave generously out of poverty. “Though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor.” || Christian giving is grounded in the grace of Christ.
9 | “God loves a cheerful giver.” Generosity leads to thanksgiving to God. || “Thanks be to God for his unspeakable gift!”
10 | Paul defends his ministry: his weapons are spiritual, “bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,” || Paul’s critics said his letters were strong but his presence weak.
11 | Paul, speaking “as a fool”, lists his sufferings: beatings, shipwrecks, dangers, sleepless nights and his anxiety for the churches. || A remarkable list of hardships, many not recorded in Acts.
12 | Paul was caught up to the third heaven, but was given “a thorn in the flesh”. God said, “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.” || What the thorn was is unknown; guesses include an illness or opponents.
13 | Paul warns he will not spare them, urges them to examine themselves, and ends with a blessing: “The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, God’s love, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all.” || The closing blessing names Father, Son and Spirit together, and is still used in worship.
