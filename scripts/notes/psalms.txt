# sourced
1 | Two ways of life: the person who delights in God’s law is like a tree planted by streams, while the wicked are like chaff blown away. || The Lord brings true happiness to those who turn from evil and delight in him [[tosn:Psalm 1:1–2]]. The apostles show that Jesus alone fulfils the ideal of the godly person in the Psalms [[tosn:Psalm 1:1–6]].
2 | The nations rage against God and his anointed king, but God has set his king on Zion: “You are my son. Today I have become your father.” || A royal psalm [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. The New Testament identifies Jesus as this anointed Son [[tosn:Psalm 2:1–12]] [[bible:Acts 13:33]] [[bible:Hebrews 1:5]].
3 | A prayer of David fleeing from Absalom: many foes surround him, yet he sleeps in peace because God sustains him. || Its title links it to David’s flight from Absalom [[bible:Psalm 3]]. The titles are early and can be taken as a reasonably reliable guide [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
4 | An evening prayer: “In peace I will both lay myself down and sleep.” || A personal lament with a prayer, a rebuke, advice and confidence [[tosn:Psalm 4:1–8]]. Feeling angry is not a sin, but anger can lead to sin [[tosn:Psalm 4:4]] [[bible:Ephesians 4:26]].
5 | A morning prayer for help against deceitful enemies, trusting God’s loving kindness to bring him into God’s house. || “Give ear to my words” is a needy person asking God, “Are you listening?” [[tosn:Psalm 5:1–2]]. Paul quotes 5:9 about human sin [[bible:Romans 3:13]].
6 | A prayer in sickness and tears: “Every night I flood my bed” God hears the sound of weeping. || The occasion may have been sickness or deep depression [[tosn:Psalm 6:1–10]]. The church has traditionally counted it among the penitential psalms, though it has no explicit confession [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
7 | A plea for justice from someone falsely accused; the wicked fall into the pit they dig. || It moves from lament and an oath of innocence to a call for justice [[tosn:Psalm 7:1–17]]. The wicked fall into the pit they dig [[bible:Psalm 7:15]].
8 | “Yahweh, our Lord, how majestic is your name in all the earth!” Under the stars David wonders what man is, that God gives him dominion over creation. || A creation psalm on the dignity of the ideal human [[tosn:Psalm 8:1–9]]. Hebrews 2:6–8 applies it to Jesus Christ [[tosn:Psalm 8:5]], and Jesus quotes 8:2 [[bible:Matthew 21:16]].
9 | Thanks for God’s justice: he is a stronghold for the oppressed and does not forget the cry of the afflicted. || Some read Psalms 9 and 10 together as one psalm in two parts [[tosn:Psalm 9:1–10:18]]. Hebrew writers used alphabet acrostics, as in Psalms 9 and 10 [[tbd:001:Acrostic]].
10 | “Why do you stand far off, Yahweh?” The wicked oppress the poor and say God will not see; the psalmist asks God to arise. || The lament of 9:13–20 continues, now with anguished questions [[tosn:Psalm 10:1–18]]. Paul quotes 10:7 [[bible:Romans 3:14]].
11 | When advised to flee like a bird, David trusts that God is on his throne and sees all. || Even when the world seems chaotic, the godly can rely on God as their refuge [[tosn:Psalm 11:1–7]]. “If the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do?” [[bible:Psalm 11:3]].
12 | People speak lies; God’s words are pure like silver refined seven times. || Evil becomes so widespread that the godly pray for rescue [[tosn:Psalm 12:1–4]]. Yahweh’s words are flawless, “as silver refined in a clay furnace” [[bible:Psalm 12:6]].
13 | “How long, Yahweh? Will you forget me forever?” Lament turns to trust and song. || “How long?” asked four times shows intense worry and pain [[tosn:Psalm 13:1–2]]. It ends in trust and song [[bible:Psalm 13:5-6]].
14 | “The fool has said in his heart, ‘There is no God.’” None does good; God looks for anyone who seeks him. || Paul uses its Greek wording to describe the wickedness of all people [[tosn:Psalm 14:1–3]] [[bible:Romans 3:10-12]]. Psalm 53 is almost the same [[bible:Psalm 53:1-6]].
15 | Who may live on God’s holy hill? The one who walks blamelessly, speaks truth and keeps promises. || A wisdom poem that invites worshippers to examine themselves [[tosn:Psalm 15:1–5]].
16 | “You will not leave my soul in Sheol, neither will you allow your holy one to see corruption.” Joy and pleasures forever in God’s presence. || A psalm of confidence [[tosn:Psalm 16:1–11]]. Peter and Paul apply 16:10 to the resurrection of Jesus [[tosn:Psalm 16:10]] [[bible:Acts 2:25-31]] [[bible:Acts 13:35]].
17 | A prayer for vindication: “Hide me under the shadow of your wings.” || The psalmist asks for God’s protection and justice and affirms his integrity [[tosn:Psalm 17:1–15]]. It ends with the hope of seeing God’s face [[bible:Psalm 17:15]].
18 | David’s great song of thanks after rescue from Saul: “Yahweh is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer.” || A similar version appears in 2 Samuel 22 [[tosn:Psalm 18:1–50]]. Its vision of praise among the nations inspired Paul’s mission to the Gentiles [[tosn:Psalm 18:49]] [[bible:Romans 15:9]].
19 | “The heavens declare the glory of God”: the sun’s course speaks without words, and God’s law is perfect, sweeter than honey. || The created world shows God’s wisdom [[tosn:Psalm 19:1–4]], and his law is perfect [[bible:Psalm 19:7]]. Paul applies 19:4 in Romans 10:18 [[tosn:Psalm 19:3–4]].
20 | A prayer for the king before battle: “Some trust in chariots, and some in horses, but we trust in the name of Yahweh our God.” || The community prays for the king’s protection and victory [[tosn:Psalm 20:1–5]]. It is one of the royal psalms [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
21 | Thanks for victory given to the king, who trusts in God. || God’s people thank him for giving the king victory [[tosn:Psalm 21:1–13]]. It is one of the royal psalms [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
22 | “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” A sufferer, mocked and surrounded, his hands and feet pierced, cries out, then praises God, who has not hidden his face. All nations will turn to God. || The apostles saw this psalm as expressing the sufferings of Christ, fulfilled at the cross [[tosn:Psalm 22:1–31]] [[bible:Mark 15:34]] [[bible:John 19:23-24]]. It moves from lament to thanksgiving [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
23 | “Yahweh is my shepherd: I shall lack nothing.” God leads to still waters, walks with us through the darkest valley, and spreads a table before us. || Ancient peoples often pictured their rulers as shepherds [[tosn:Psalm 23:1–3]]. It brings to mind the Good Shepherd and the messianic banquet [[tosn:Psalm 23:1–6]] [[bible:John 10:11]].
24 | “The earth is Yahweh’s, with its fullness.” Who may ascend his hill? “Lift up your heads, you gates… the King of glory will come in.” || The earth belongs to the Lord who made it [[tosn:Psalm 24:1]], and Paul quotes 24:1 [[bible:1 Corinthians 10:26]]. “Clean hands and a pure heart” compares with Matthew 5:8 [[tosn:Psalm 24:4]].
25 | An alphabetic prayer for guidance and forgiveness: “Show me your ways, Yahweh. Teach me your paths.” || An acrostic, one of the alphabet poems of the Bible [[tbd:001:Acrostic]], combining lament and wisdom teaching [[tosn:Psalm 25:1–22]].
26 | A plea of integrity: “Examine me, Yahweh, and prove me.” The psalmist loves God’s house. || A personal lament asking for redemption because of his innocence and the Lord’s justice [[tosn:Psalm 26:1–12]].
27 | “Yahweh is my light and my salvation. Whom shall I fear?” One thing he asks: to live in God’s house and see his beauty. || Confidence and lament united by longing for the Lord’s presence [[tosn:Psalm 27:1–14]]. It ends, “Wait for Yahweh” [[bible:Psalm 27:14]].
28 | A cry for help that turns to thanks: “Yahweh is my strength and my shield.” || A personal lament asking for justice and mercy [[tosn:Psalm 28:1–9]]. Laments often turn to thanksgiving for the deliverance sought, as here [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
29 | The voice of Yahweh thunders over the waters, breaks the cedars and shakes the wilderness; God gives his people peace. || It calls the heavenly beings to honour the Lord for his power [[tosn:Psalm 29:1–2]]. “Yahweh’s voice” sounds seven times [[bible:Psalm 29:3-9]].
30 | Thanks for healing: “Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning.” “You have turned my mourning into dancing.” || The psalmist praises God for rescue, like being saved from death [[tosn:Psalm 30:1–3]]. Its title links it to the dedication of the temple [[bible:Psalm 30]].
31 | “Into your hand I commend my spirit.” Trust in God in trouble and slander. || Jesus spoke 31:5 on the cross before he died [[tosn:Psalm 31:5]] [[bible:Luke 23:46]]. Stephen prays in similar words [[bible:Acts 7:59]].
32 | “Blessed is he whose disobedience is forgiven” When David kept silent his strength dried up; when he confessed, God forgave. || God’s forgiveness removes guilt and brings joy, and Paul quotes 32:1–2 when explaining justification by faith [[tosn:Psalm 32:1–2]] [[bible:Romans 4:7-8]]. It is one of the clearest penitential psalms [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
33 | Sing a new song: by God’s word the heavens were made. No king is saved by a large army; God’s eye is on those who fear him. || A call to praise with skilful music and a new song [[tosn:Psalm 33:1–3]]. “By Yahweh’s word, the heavens were made” [[bible:Psalm 33:6]].
34 | “Oh taste and see that Yahweh is good.” The angel of Yahweh camps around those who fear him; he is near the broken-hearted. || An acrostic [[tbd:001:Acrostic]]. Its title names Abimelech, while 1 Samuel names Achish [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. Peter quotes 34:12–16, and John applies 34:20 to Jesus [[tosn:Psalm 34:12–16]] [[tosn:Psalm 34:20]].
35 | A plea for God to fight those who repay good with evil and hate without cause. || The Lord’s troubled servant asks God to defend him [[tosn:Psalm 35:1–28]]. John applies “hated me without a cause” to Jesus [[tosn:Psalm 35:19]] [[bible:John 15:25]].
36 | The wicked have no fear of God, but God’s loving kindness reaches to the heavens: “In your light we will see light.” || It contrasts the foolish world of the wicked with the world God made [[tosn:Psalm 36:1–12]]. Paul uses “no fear of God” in Romans 3:18 [[tosn:Psalm 36:1]].
37 | “Don’t fret because of evildoers.” Trust in God, delight in him, commit your way to him; “the humble shall inherit the land.” || A wisdom acrostic [[tbd:001:Acrostic]] on trusting God instead of envying evildoers [[tosn:Psalm 37:1–9]]. 37:11 compares with Jesus’s words in Matthew 5:5 [[tosn:Psalm 37:11]] [[bible:Matthew 5:5]].
38 | A sick and guilty man, abandoned by friends, waits for God. || A lament and prayer for healing [[tosn:Psalm 38:1–22]], one of the clearest penitential psalms [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. The psalmist links his sickness with his sin [[tosn:Psalm 38:1–4]].
39 | Life is a breath: “Yahweh, show me my end… how frail I am.” || Suffering in silence makes his anguish worse [[tosn:Psalm 39:1–3]]. “Show me my end… how frail I am” [[bible:Psalm 39:4]].
40 | God lifted the psalmist from the pit and put a new song in his mouth. “Sacrifice and offering you didn’t desire… I delight to do your will.” || Thanksgiving followed by lament [[tosn:Psalm 40:1–17]]. Hebrews 10:5–7 quotes the Greek version of 40:6–8 [[tosn:Psalm 40:6]].
41 | Blessed is the one who considers the poor. Even a close friend who ate bread with him has turned against him. || A wisdom psalm asking for healing [[tosn:Psalm 41:1–13]]. Jesus applies 41:9 to his betrayal [[tosn:Psalm 41:9]] [[bible:John 13:18]]. A doxology at 41:13 closes the first of the five books [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
42 | “As the deer pants for the water brooks, so my soul pants after you, God.” Far from the temple, the psalmist asks, “Why are you in despair, my soul?… Hope in God!” || Psalms 42 and 43 form a unit with a shared refrain of hope [[tosn:Psalm 42:1–43:5]]. The second book begins here [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]], and the title links it to the sons of Korah [[bible:Psalm 42]].
43 | “Send out your light and your truth. Let them lead me” back to God’s altar. || The psalmist asks for rescue, guidance and restoration [[tosn:Psalm 43:1–4]]; the refrain of Psalm 42 returns [[bible:Psalm 43:5]].
44 | A national lament: God gave victory in the past, but now Israel is defeated though faithful. “Wake up! Why do you sleep, Lord?” || A national lament after a defeat in battle [[tosn:Psalm 44:1–26]]. Paul quotes 44:22 in Romans 8:36 [[tosn:Psalm 44:22]].
45 | A wedding song for the king: “Your throne, God, is forever and ever.” || A royal wedding psalm [[tosn:Psalm 45:1–17]]. Hebrews uses 45:6–7 to show the Son is greater than the angels [[tosn:Psalm 45:6–9]] [[bible:Hebrews 1:8-9]].
46 | “God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.” Though the earth shakes, “Be still, and know that I am God.” || A song of Zion [[tosn:Psalm 48:1–14]]. The river recalls the streams of Eden and Ezekiel 47 [[tosn:Psalm 46:4]], and “with us” is part of the name Immanuel [[tosn:Psalm 46:7]].
47 | Clap your hands, all peoples: God is King of all the earth and has gone up with a shout. || As ruler of all nations, God deserves praise [[tosn:Psalm 47:1–2]]. “God has gone up with a shout” [[bible:Psalm 47:5]].
48 | Mount Zion, the city of the great King, is beautiful; God is her fortress. “Walk about Zion… Number its towers.” || A song of Zion, the city of the Great King [[tosn:Psalm 48:1–14]]. Jesus calls Jerusalem “the city of the great King” [[bible:Matthew 5:35]].
49 | Wealth cannot ransom a life from death, “but God will redeem my soul from the power of Sheol.” || Anyone who will listen can gain wisdom [[tosn:Psalm 49:1–2]]. “God will redeem my soul from the power of Sheol” [[bible:Psalm 49:15]].
50 | God summons his people to judgement: he does not need their sacrifices but wants thanks, faithfulness and honesty. || God comes to judge [[tosn:Psalm 50:1–3]]. True sacrifice is dedicating oneself to God [[tosn:Psalm 50:14]], and ritual without justice and faithfulness is empty [[tosn:Psalm 50:17]].
51 | David’s prayer after his sin with Bathsheba: “Have mercy on me, God… Create in me a clean heart.” “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit.” || The title links it to Nathan’s visit after David’s sin with Bathsheba [[bible:Psalm 51]], a close match with 2 Samuel 11–12 [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. It is one of the clearest penitential psalms [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. Hyssop was used for cleansing rites [[tosn:Psalm 51:7]].
52 | Against a boastful, lying man; the psalmist is “like a green olive tree in God’s house”. || Its title links it to Doeg the Edomite [[bible:Psalm 52]] [[bible:1 Samuel 22:9-10]]. Liars deceive themselves into thinking their words make them heroes [[tosn:Psalm 52:1–4]].
53 | “The fool has said in his heart, ‘There is no God.’” || Almost the same as Psalm 14 [[bible:Psalm 14:1-7]]; Paul uses its Greek wording in Romans 3:10–12 [[tosn:Psalm 53:1–3]].
54 | A short prayer for deliverance from strangers who seek his life. || Its title links it to the Ziphites betraying David [[bible:Psalm 54]]. The psalmist prays for God’s help against violent, godless people [[tosn:Psalm 54:1–7]].
55 | Betrayed by a close friend, the psalmist wishes for the wings of a dove to fly away. “Cast your burden on Yahweh, and he will sustain you.” || The occasion is an attack by an enemy [[tosn:Psalm 55:1–3]]; Jesus went through similar pain when his friend Judas betrayed him [[tosn:Psalm 55:1–23]]. 1 Peter 5:7 echoes 55:22 [[bible:1 Peter 5:7]].
56 | “When I am afraid, I will put my trust in you… What can man do to me?” God keeps his tears in a bottle. || Its title links it to the Philistines seizing David in Gath [[bible:Psalm 56]]. Twice it moves from asking for help to confidence [[tosn:Psalm 56:1–13]].
57 | In the cave, hiding from Saul, David takes refuge in the shadow of God’s wings and wakes the dawn with song. || Its title places it in the cave as David fled from Saul [[bible:Psalm 57]]. Verses 7–11 reappear in Psalm 108 [[tosn:Psalm 108:1–13]].
58 | A fierce plea against corrupt judges who do injustice. || The judges were serving themselves instead of the needy [[tosn:Psalm 58:1–5]]. Psalms with curses on enemies are called imprecatory psalms [[tbd:009:Imprecatory Psalms]].
59 | Enemies prowl the city like dogs at night; God is the psalmist’s fortress. || Its title links it to Saul’s men watching David’s house [[bible:Psalm 59]] [[bible:1 Samuel 19:11]].
60 | After defeat, the people pray: “Give us help against the adversary, for the help of man is vain.” || When God’s people feel his discipline, they stay loyal and wait for his answer [[tosn:Psalm 60:1–4]]. Verses 5–12 reappear in Psalm 108 [[tosn:Psalm 108:1–13]].
61 | “Lead me to the rock that is higher than I.” A prayer for the king. || The psalmist asks to be brought into God’s presence and prays for the king [[tosn:Psalm 61:1–8]]. It is one of the royal psalms [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
62 | “My soul rests in God alone.” Power belongs to God, and loving kindness too. || A psalm of trust in which the king relies on God in hard times [[tosn:Psalm 62:1–12]].
63 | In a dry and weary land, “my soul thirsts for you… your loving kindness is better than life.” || Its title places David in the desert of Judah [[bible:Psalm 63]]. The king longs for God’s presence and finds strength in him [[tosn:Psalm 63:1–11]].
64 | Enemies plot with tongues like swords, but God shoots his arrow and they stumble. || A lament against the plans and arrogance of the wicked [[tosn:Psalm 64:1–10]].
65 | Praise for God who forgives, stills the seas, and waters the earth: the meadows are clothed with flocks. || Praise for God’s acts in redemption and in nature [[tosn:Psalm 65:1–3]]; the streams and harvests show his goodness [[tosn:Psalm 65:9]].
66 | “Make a joyful shout to God, all the earth!” Remember how God brought Israel through fire and water. || The psalmist calls all the earth to praise God’s glory [[tosn:Psalm 66:1–4]].
67 | “May God be merciful to us, bless us, and cause his face to shine on us,” so that all nations may know his salvation. || Inspired by the priestly blessing in Numbers 6:24–26, so that God becomes known among the nations [[tosn:Psalm 67:1–2]].
68 | God marches from Sinai to Zion in victory, “a father of the fatherless, and a defender of the widows”. || Paul applies 68:18 to Christ [[tosn:Psalm 68:18]] [[bible:Ephesians 4:8]]. It opens like Israel’s march into the land [[tosn:Psalm 68:1–3]].
69 | “The waters have come up to my neck.” “the zeal of your house consumes me”; “In my thirst, they gave me vinegar to drink.” || John applies 69:4 and 69:9 to Jesus [[tosn:Psalm 69:4]] [[tosn:Psalm 69:9]] [[bible:John 2:17]]. A personal lament of deep pain [[tosn:Psalm 69:1–36]].
70 | “Hurry, God, to deliver me.” || Almost exactly the same as Psalm 40:13–17 [[tosn:Psalm 70:1–5]].
71 | An old person’s prayer: “Don’t reject me in my old age. Don’t forsake me when my strength fails.” || An untitled lament by an ageing believer, more a confession of confidence than a cry of abandonment [[tosn:Psalm 71:1–24]].
72 | A prayer for the king: may he defend the poor, and may his rule reach from sea to sea, with kings bringing gifts. || The title is “By Solomon” [[bible:Psalm 72]]. Its picture of perfect rule hints at Jesus, the Son of David [[tosn:Psalm 72:1–20]]. A doxology closes the second book [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]] [[bible:Psalm 72:18-20]].
73 | Asaph almost slipped when he envied the prosperous wicked, until he entered God’s sanctuary: “Whom do I have in heaven? There is no one on earth whom I desire besides you” || A wisdom psalm on the prosperity of the wicked [[tosn:Psalm 73:1–28]]. The third book begins, and it is one of the twelve psalms linked with Asaph [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
74 | A lament over the destroyed temple: “Why have you rejected us forever?” God crushed Leviathan; let him remember now. || Written in the aftermath of Jerusalem’s fall [[tosn:Psalm 74:1–2]].
75 | God says, “When I choose the appointed time, I will judge blameless.” He holds the cup of judgement. || A thanksgiving hymn with two messages from God assuring his justice [[tosn:Psalm 75:1–10]]. He raises up and brings down [[tosn:Psalm 75:7–8]].
76 | God is known in Judah; he broke the weapons of war and judges to save the afflicted. || A song of Zion [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. God’s victory over Zion’s enemies hints at his future victory over all enemies [[tosn:Psalm 76:1–3]].
77 | In distress the psalmist cannot sleep and wonders if God has forgotten, then remembers God leading his people through the sea. || The night is a time for prayer and meditation [[tosn:Psalm 77:1–3]]. Remembering the sea crossing gives hope [[bible:Psalm 77:19-20]].
78 | A long history lesson: Israel’s repeated rebellion in the wilderness and God’s patience, ending with God choosing David as shepherd. || Jesus quoted 78:2 to explain why he taught in parables [[tosn:Psalm 78:2]] [[bible:Matthew 13:35]]. The introduction invites people to learn wisdom [[tosn:Psalm 78:1–8]].
79 | “The nations have come into your inheritance; they have defiled your holy temple.” A plea for help and forgiveness. || A lament over the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC [[tosn:Psalm 79:1–13]].
80 | “Turn us again, God. Cause your face to shine, and we will be saved.” Israel is a vine brought out of Egypt, now broken down. || God is called the Shepherd of Israel [[tosn:Psalm 80:1–2]], and Israel a fruitful vine [[tosn:Psalm 80:8–9]].
81 | A festival call to sing, with God’s word: “Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it.” || Before warning Israel, the psalmist calls them to song and dance [[tosn:Psalm 81:1–3]]. “Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it” [[bible:Psalm 81:10]].
82 | God stands in the divine council and judges the “gods” who rule unjustly: “Defend the weak, the poor, and the fatherless.” || God condemns those who mistreat the helpless [[tosn:Psalm 82:1–8]]. Jesus quotes 82:6 in John 10:34 [[tosn:Psalm 82:6–7]].
83 | A prayer against a coalition of nations plotting to destroy Israel. || A community lament asking God to rescue his people [[tosn:Psalm 83:1–18]].
84 | “How lovely are your dwellings, Yahweh of Armies!” Even the sparrow has found a home near God’s altar; a day in his courts is better than a thousand. || The psalmist is far from the temple and longs for it [[tosn:Psalm 84:1–4]]. It is a song of Zion [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
85 | God restored his people before; may he do it again. “Mercy and truth meet together. Righteousness and peace have kissed each other.” || The community back from exile prays for complete restoration [[tosn:Psalm 85:1–13]].
86 | “Teach me your way, Yahweh. I will walk in your truth. Make my heart undivided.” || God’s devoted servant prays for mercy [[tosn:Psalm 86:1–4]]. 86:15 repeats God’s self-description in Exodus 34:6 [[bible:Psalm 86:15]] [[bible:Exodus 34:6]].
87 | Zion is the birthplace of the nations: Egypt, Babylon, Philistia and Tyre will be counted as born in her. || Jerusalem is the city of God, where people from all nations are counted as citizens [[tosn:Psalm 87:1–7]].
88 | The darkest psalm: a lifelong sufferer prays from the edge of death, and it ends in darkness, with no word of comfort. || The psalmist sees himself cast out from God’s presence [[tosn:Psalm 88:1–18]]. Its title names Heman with the sons of Korah [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
89 | God’s covenant with David is praised at length, but now the king is defeated: “Lord, where are your former loving kindnesses?” || Book Three starts and ends with serious questions: praise for David’s throne turns to lament [[tosn:Psalm 89:1–52]]. The title names Ethan [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
90 | A prayer of Moses: “Lord, you have been our dwelling place for all generations.” A thousand years are like yesterday to God. “Teach us to count our days.” || The only psalm credited to Moses [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. The fourth book begins here [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]]. The Lord is the dwelling place of the godly [[tosn:Psalm 90:1–2]].
91 | “He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High will rest in the shadow of the Almighty.” Angels will guard you. || Satan quoted 91:11 when tempting Jesus, but the promise is for those who obey, not those who test God [[tosn:Psalm 91:11]] [[bible:Matthew 4:6]].
92 | A song for the Sabbath: “It is a good thing to give thanks to Yahweh.” The righteous flourish like the palm tree. || The only psalm whose title marks it for the Sabbath [[tosn:Psalm 92:1]].
93 | “Yahweh reigns! He is clothed with majesty.” Higher than the roar of the sea. || The everlasting Lord establishes his kingship [[tosn:Psalm 93:1–5]]; Psalms 93–100 answer the doubts raised by the exile [[tosn:Psalm 93:1–100:5]].
94 | “you God to whom vengeance belongs, shine out.” How long will the wicked triumph? “In the multitude of my thoughts within me, your comforts delight my soul.” || The community prays for the Lord to bring justice [[tosn:Psalm 94:1–7]]. Paul quotes 94:11 [[tosn:Psalm 94:11]] [[bible:1 Corinthians 3:20]].
95 | “Oh come, let’s sing to Yahweh… Let’s kneel before Yahweh, our Maker.” Today, do not harden your hearts as at Meribah. || A call to worship and to follow the Lord faithfully [[tosn:Psalm 95:1–11]]. Hebrews 3:7–4:13 builds on it [[tosn:Psalm 95:11]], and Hebrews 4:7 credits it to David [[tbd:016:Psalms, Book of]].
96 | “Sing to Yahweh a new song! Sing to Yahweh, all the earth.” Declare his glory among the nations; he comes to judge with righteousness. || It proclaims the Lord’s kingship over all and his coming to judge with justice [[tosn:Psalm 96:1–13]].
