1 | The risen Jesus tells his followers to wait for the Holy Spirit: “You will be witnesses… to the uttermost parts of the earth.” He is taken up into heaven. Matthias replaces Judas. || 1:8 gives the outline of Acts: Jerusalem, Judea and Samaria, the ends of the earth.
2 | At Pentecost the Spirit comes like wind and fire, and the disciples speak in other languages. Peter preaches, and three thousand believe. The believers share everything. || Peter quotes Joel 2 and Psalms 16 and 110. Many see Pentecost as reversing Babel. The church’s four marks: teaching, fellowship, breaking bread and prayer (2:42).
3 | Peter and John heal a man lame from birth at the temple gate: “What I have, that I give you.” Peter preaches about Jesus, the prophet like Moses. || “I have no silver or gold”: healing in the name of Jesus.
4 | Peter and John are arrested and boldly tell the council, “There is salvation in no one else.” The believers pray, the place shakes, and they share possessions. || The leaders notice they are “unlearned and ignorant men” who had been with Jesus. Barnabas is introduced.
5 | Ananias and Sapphira lie about a gift and die. The apostles are arrested, freed by an angel, and say, “We must obey God rather than men.” Gamaliel advises caution. || Gamaliel was Paul’s teacher (22:3). The apostles rejoice to suffer for the Name.
6 | Seven men, including Stephen, are chosen to care for neglected Greek-speaking widows. Stephen is arrested. || An early example of solving injustice in the church. The seven all have Greek names.
7 | Stephen retells Israel’s history, accusing the leaders of resisting the Holy Spirit. He sees Jesus standing at God’s right hand and is stoned, praying, “Lord, don’t hold this sin against them!” Saul approves. || The first Christian martyr. Jesus “standing” to welcome him; Stephen’s prayer echoes Jesus’s on the cross.
8 | Persecution scatters the believers. Philip preaches in Samaria, and later explains Isaiah 53 to an Ethiopian official, who is baptized. || The good news reaches Samaria and Africa, as 1:8 foretold.
9 | On the road to Damascus Saul is blinded by a light and hears Jesus: “Saul, why do you persecute me?” Ananias lays hands on him. Peter heals Aeneas and raises Tabitha. || Paul’s conversion is told three times in Acts (9, 22, 26).
10 | Peter sees a vision of unclean animals: “What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean.” He visits the Roman centurion Cornelius, and the Spirit falls on Gentiles. || “God doesn’t show favouritism.” The turning point for welcoming Gentiles into the church.
11 | Peter explains to Jerusalem what happened. In Antioch many Greeks believe, and the disciples are first called “Christians”. || Antioch becomes the base for mission to the Gentiles.
12 | Herod Agrippa kills James and imprisons Peter, who is freed by an angel while the church prays. Herod dies. || Rhoda leaves Peter knocking at the door in her joy. Josephus also describes Herod Agrippa I’s sudden death in AD 44.
13 | Barnabas and Saul are sent out from Antioch on the first missionary journey. In Pisidian Antioch Paul preaches in the synagogue and turns to the Gentiles. || From here Saul is called Paul. The pattern begins: Jews first, then Gentiles.
14 | In Iconium, Lystra and Derbe, Paul and Barnabas are mistaken for gods, then Paul is stoned and left for dead, but gets up and goes on. || “Through many afflictions we must enter into God’s Kingdom.”
15 | The council at Jerusalem decides Gentile believers need not be circumcised or keep the whole law of Moses. Paul and Barnabas part ways over John Mark. || James quotes Amos 9. A landmark for a church of Jews and Gentiles together.
16 | With Silas and Timothy, Paul is called in a vision to Macedonia. Lydia believes in Philippi; Paul and Silas are jailed, sing at midnight, and the jailer believes. || “We” sections begin (16:10), suggesting Luke joined Paul. The good news reaches Europe.
17 | In Thessalonica and Berea, then in Athens, Paul speaks at the Areopagus about the “unknown god”, quoting Greek poets. || The Bereans examined the Scriptures daily. Paul builds bridges to Greek culture.
18 | In Corinth Paul stays eighteen months, making tents with Aquila and Priscilla. Apollos is taught the way of God more accurately. || Gallio’s term as proconsul (about AD 51–52), known from an inscription at Delphi, helps date Paul’s life.
19 | In Ephesus Paul teaches for two years; magic books are burned; a riot breaks out led by silversmiths of Artemis. || The theatre of Ephesus, where the crowd shouted for two hours, can still be visited.
20 | Paul travels through Macedonia and Greece. In Troas Eutychus falls from a window and is raised. Paul says farewell to the Ephesian elders: “It is more blessed to give than to receive.” || The only saying of Jesus quoted outside the Gospels in the New Testament (20:35).
21 | Paul goes to Jerusalem despite warnings, is attacked in the temple, and is arrested by the Romans. || Agabus binds Paul’s hands with his belt as a sign.
22 | Paul tells the crowd his conversion story; when he mentions the Gentiles they riot. He is a Roman citizen and avoids flogging. || Paul trained under Gamaliel in Jerusalem.
23 | Before the council Paul divides Pharisees and Sadducees over the resurrection. A plot is uncovered, and he is sent to the governor Felix in Caesarea. || Paul’s nephew warns of the plot. Jesus tells Paul, “You must testify also at Rome.”
24 | Paul defends himself before Felix, who keeps him in prison for two years, hoping for a bribe. || Paul speaks to Felix about “righteousness, self-control, and the judgement to come”, and Felix is afraid.
25 | Before the new governor Festus, Paul appeals to Caesar. King Agrippa wants to hear him. || The appeal sets the road to Rome.
26 | Paul tells King Agrippa his story. Agrippa says, “With a little persuasion are you trying to make me a Christian?” || Paul’s defence centres on the resurrection as the hope of Israel.
27 | Sailing to Rome, Paul’s ship is caught in a storm for two weeks. Paul encourages everyone, and all 276 people reach land safely when the ship wrecks. || A vivid sea narrative with accurate nautical detail.
28 | On Malta Paul survives a snakebite and heals many. In Rome he lives under house arrest for two years, “preaching God’s Kingdom… with all boldness, without hindrance.” || Acts ends open, with the gospel freely proclaimed in the capital of the empire. Tradition says Paul was later martyred under Nero.
