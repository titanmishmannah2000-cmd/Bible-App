# sourced
1 | Amos, a shepherd from Tekoa, announces God’s judgement on Israel’s neighbours, “for three transgressions… yes, for four”: Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom and Ammon. || Amos received his vision “two years before the earthquake” (1:1); Zechariah 14:5 places it under Uzziah, and Hazor may show an earthquake around 760 BC [[tbd:002:Book of Amos]]. Amos treats it as God’s judgement [[tosn:Amos 1:1]].
2 | Judgement on Moab and Judah, then on Israel itself: they sell the righteous for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals. || The nations sinned against basic human decency, but Judah went further: they had God’s law and rejected it [[tosn:Amos 2:4]].
3 | “I have only chosen you of all the families of the earth. Therefore I will punish you” “Do two walk together, unless they have agreed?” || Being chosen will not protect Israel from judgement; they are held to a higher standard [[tosn:Amos 3:1–2]].
4 | The rich women of Samaria are “cows of Bashan” who oppress the poor. God sent disasters, “yet you haven’t returned to me”. “Prepare to meet your God.” || The “cows of Bashan” are Israel’s wealthy wives who ignored the poor [[tosn:Amos 4:1]]. Each disaster ends with “yet you haven’t returned to me” [[tosn:Amos 4:6–11]].
5 | “Seek me, and you will live.” God hates their festivals: “Let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream.” || God did not want empty offerings but justice flowing like water (5:18–24) [[tbd:002:Book of Amos]]. The day of the Lord would bring darkness, fulfilled when Assyria conquered Israel in 722 BC [[tosn:Amos 5:18]]. Stephen quotes 5:25–27 in Acts 7:42–43 [[bible:Acts 7:42-43]].
6 | Woe to those at ease in Zion, lounging on ivory beds while not grieving for the ruin of their people. || Excavations at Samaria have uncovered many ivory decorations, matching Amos’s picture of the rich (6:4) [[tbd:002:Book of Amos]]. Including Zion shows God has no favourites [[tosn:Amos 6:1]].
7 | Visions of locusts, fire and a plumb line. Amaziah the priest tells Amos to go home; Amos replies, “I was no prophet… I was a herdsman.” || In the first two visions God withdraws judgement after Amos pleads [[tosn:Amos 7:1–6]]. Jeroboam’s son Zechariah was murdered within a year [[tosn:Amos 7:9]].
8 | A basket of summer fruit means the end has come. Merchants cheat the poor. A famine is coming, “not a famine of bread… but of hearing Yahweh’s words.” || A wordplay: summer fruit (qayits) and “the end” (qets) [[tosn:Amos 8:2]]. An eclipse around 763 BC may lie behind 8:9 [[tbd:002:Book of Amos]].
9 | No one escapes God’s judgement, but he will not utterly destroy. “In that day I will raise up the tent of David who is fallen,” and the mountains will drip with new wine. || God’s kingdom will welcome the nations, as promised to Abraham [[tosn:Amos 9:12]]. James quotes 9:11–12 at the Jerusalem council [[bible:Acts 15:16-17]].
