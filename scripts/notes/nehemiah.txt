# sourced
1 | In Persia Nehemiah, the king’s cupbearer, hears that Jerusalem’s walls lie broken. He weeps, fasts and prays. || His prayer has praise, confession, remembering God’s promises and a request [[tosn:Nehemiah 1:5–11]]; the promise to gather the scattered echoes Deuteronomy 30 [[bible:Nehemiah 1:8-9]] [[bible:Deuteronomy 30:1-4]].
2 | King Artaxerxes notices Nehemiah’s sadness and sends him to rebuild Jerusalem. He inspects the walls by night. || Asked “What is your request?”, Nehemiah first “prayed to the God of heaven” [[bible:Nehemiah 2:4]].
3 | Families each rebuild a section of the wall and its gates. || Men and women from many towns helped, including priests, goldsmiths and perfume makers [[bsn:Nehemiah 3:1–7:3]].
4 | Sanballat and Tobiah mock and threaten; the builders work with one hand and hold a weapon in the other. || “Our God will fight for us” draws on Israel’s old tradition that the LORD fights for his people [[tosn:Nehemiah 4:20]] [[bible:Exodus 14:14]].
5 | Nehemiah stops wealthy Jews from charging interest and enslaving poor neighbours, and refuses his governor’s allowance. || Nehemiah lent without charging interest, as the law required, and made the lenders give back what they had taken [[tosn:Nehemiah 5:10]] [[tosn:Nehemiah 5:11]].
6 | Enemies try to trap and frighten Nehemiah, but the wall is finished in fifty-two days. || The enemies “perceived that this work was done by our God” [[bible:Nehemiah 6:16]].
7 | Gatekeepers are appointed, and Nehemiah finds the list of the first returning families. || The list is like Ezra 2, with some differences in names and numbers [[tosn:Nehemiah 7:8–38]].
8 | Ezra reads the Law from a wooden platform from dawn to noon while Levites explain it. The people weep, then celebrate the Feast of Tabernacles. || The Levites probably explained the Hebrew Scriptures in Aramaic, the language most people spoke [[tosn:Nehemiah 8:7–9]]. “The joy of Yahweh is your strength” [[bible:Nehemiah 8:10]].
9 | The people fast and confess, and the Levites lead a long prayer retelling Israel’s story of sin and God’s mercy. || The long prayer confesses the nation’s sins and praises God’s compassion throughout its history [[tosn:Nehemiah 9:5–37]]: “a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful” [[bible:Nehemiah 9:17]].
10 | The leaders sign a covenant to keep God’s law: no mixed marriages, keep the Sabbath, support the temple. || The promise ends, “We will not forsake the house of our God” [[bible:Nehemiah 10:39]].
11 | One in ten people move into Jerusalem to repopulate the city. || “The people blessed all the men who willingly offered themselves to dwell in Jerusalem” [[bible:Nehemiah 11:2]].
12 | Priests and Levites are listed, and the wall is dedicated with two great choirs. || God was the source of their joy, and Psalm 147 may have been written for this occasion [[tosn:Nehemiah 12:43]]. “The joy of Jerusalem was heard even far away” [[bible:Nehemiah 12:43]].
13 | Returning after an absence, Nehemiah finds the reforms neglected: Tobiah in the temple, the Sabbath broken, mixed marriages. He acts firmly. || Nehemiah repeatedly asks God to remember him, probably because he faced opposition from those he corrected [[tosn:Nehemiah 13:14]]. The book ends, “Remember me, my God, for good” [[bible:Nehemiah 13:31]].
