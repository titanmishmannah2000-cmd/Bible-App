1 | “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… The Word became flesh, and lived among us.” John the Baptist points to Jesus: “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!” The first disciples follow. || The prologue echoes Genesis 1. Andrew brings his brother Simon; Nathanael is promised a vision of angels ascending and descending on the Son of Man.
2 | At a wedding in Cana Jesus turns water into wine, his first sign. In Jerusalem he clears the temple: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” || John places the temple clearing at the start of Jesus’s ministry, the other Gospels at the end. The temple Jesus meant was his body.
3 | Nicodemus visits Jesus at night and is told he must be born again (or “from above”). “For God so loved the world, that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life.” || The Greek anothen means both “again” and “from above”. 3:16 is perhaps the best-known verse in the Bible.
4 | At Jacob’s well Jesus asks a Samaritan woman for water and offers her “living water”. She brings her town to him. He heals an official’s son. || Jesus breaks barriers of race, gender and morality. “God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.”
5 | Jesus heals a man paralysed for thirty-eight years at the pool of Bethesda, on the Sabbath. He says he does what he sees the Father doing. || The pool with five porches has been excavated in Jerusalem, matching John’s description.
6 | Jesus feeds five thousand and walks on the sea. He says, “I am the bread of life.” Many disciples leave, but Peter says, “Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life.” || The first of seven “I am” sayings. Christians read the bread discourse in light of the Lord’s Supper.
7 | At the Feast of Tabernacles Jesus teaches in the temple: “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink.” People argue whether he is the Christ. || Living water flowing from within echoes Ezekiel 47 and Zechariah 14.
8 | A woman caught in adultery: “He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone.” Jesus says, “I am the light of the world,” and “Before Abraham came into existence, I AM.” || The adultery story (7:53–8:11) is missing from the earliest manuscripts but widely accepted as a genuine memory of Jesus. “I AM” echoes God’s name in Exodus 3:14.
9 | Jesus heals a man born blind, who is questioned and thrown out of the synagogue. “I was blind, now I see.” || Jesus rejects the idea that the blindness was punishment for sin. Spiritual blindness belongs to those who claim to see.
10 | “I am the door.” “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” “I and the Father are one.” || Draws on Ezekiel 34. Jesus has “other sheep”, a hint of the Gentiles.
11 | Lazarus dies. Jesus tells Martha, “I am the resurrection and the life.” Jesus weeps, then calls Lazarus out of the tomb. The leaders plot to kill Jesus. || “Jesus wept” is the shortest verse in most English Bibles. Caiaphas unknowingly prophesies that one man should die for the people.
12 | Mary anoints Jesus’s feet. Jesus enters Jerusalem to cries of Hosanna. Greeks ask to see him. “Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone.” || The arrival of Gentiles signals “the hour” has come.
13 | At the last supper Jesus washes his disciples’ feet. Judas leaves into the night. “A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another… as I have loved you.” || Foot washing is still practised on Maundy Thursday (from mandatum, “commandment”).
14 | “Don’t let your heart be troubled… In my Father’s house are many homes.” “I am the way, the truth, and the life.” Jesus promises the Holy Spirit, the Counsellor, and gives his peace. || Many readers find comfort here at funerals. The Spirit is called the Paraclete: advocate, helper, comforter.
15 | “I am the true vine… you are the branches.” Remain in my love. “Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends.” || The vine recalls Israel as God’s vine (Psalm 80; Isaiah 5).
16 | The Spirit will convict the world and guide the disciples into all truth. Their grief will turn to joy. “In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world.” || Jesus compares the disciples’ sorrow to a woman in labour whose pain turns to joy.
17 | Jesus prays for himself, for his disciples, and for all who will believe through them, “that they may all be one”. “This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God.” || Often called Jesus’s “high priestly prayer”, and a key text for Christian unity.
18 | Jesus is arrested in a garden, Peter cuts off a servant’s ear, Jesus is questioned by Annas and Caiaphas, Peter denies him, and Pilate asks, “What is truth?” || When Jesus says “I am”, the soldiers fall back. Jesus says his kingdom is not of this world.
19 | Jesus is flogged and crowned with thorns; Pilate says, “Behold, the man!” He is crucified, entrusts his mother to the beloved disciple, says “It is finished,” and dies. Blood and water flow from his side. || “It is finished” (tetelestai) can mean “completed” or “paid in full”. John notes fulfilled Scriptures about his clothing, his bones and his pierced side.
20 | Mary Magdalene finds the tomb empty; the risen Jesus calls her by name. He appears to the disciples, breathes the Spirit on them, and later shows Thomas his wounds: “My Lord and my God!” || John states his purpose: “that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name.”
21 | By the Sea of Galilee the risen Jesus gives a great catch of fish and cooks breakfast. Three times he asks Peter, “Do you love me?” and says, “Feed my sheep.” || An epilogue. Peter’s threefold restoration matches his threefold denial; the 153 fish have intrigued readers for centuries.
