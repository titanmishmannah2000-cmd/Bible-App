1 | Luke writes an orderly account for Theophilus. The angel Gabriel announces the birth of John to old Zechariah, and of Jesus to Mary, who answers, “Let it be done to me according to your word.” Mary visits Elizabeth and sings the Magnificat; John is born. || Mary’s song echoes Hannah’s (1 Samuel 2) and celebrates God lifting the lowly. Zechariah’s song is the Benedictus.
2 | Jesus is born in Bethlehem and laid in a manger; angels announce good news to shepherds. He is presented in the temple, where Simeon and Anna recognize him. At twelve he stays behind in his Father’s house. || Simeon’s song (Nunc Dimittis) calls Jesus “a light for revelation to the nations”. Shepherds were humble, often looked down on.
3 | John the Baptizer preaches repentance and sharing. Jesus is baptized and the Spirit descends. Luke gives Jesus’s family tree back to Adam, “the son of God”. || Luke traces Jesus to Adam, not only Abraham: he belongs to all humanity. Dated to the fifteenth year of Tiberius, about AD 28–29.
4 | Jesus is tempted in the wilderness. In the Nazareth synagogue he reads Isaiah 61, “The Spirit of the Lord is on me… to preach good news to the poor” and says, “Today this Scripture has been fulfilled.” The town tries to kill him. || Jesus’s mission statement in Luke. He mentions God’s care for the widow of Zarephath and Naaman, both foreigners, which angers the crowd.
5 | A miraculous catch of fish; Peter says, “Depart from me, for I am a sinful man.” Jesus heals a leper and a paralysed man, calls Levi, and says he came to call sinners to repentance. || “new wine must be put into fresh wine skins”
6 | Sabbath conflicts; Jesus chooses the Twelve after a night of prayer. The Sermon on the Plain: blessings on the poor and woes on the rich; love your enemies; build on the rock. || Luke’s version of the Beatitudes is shorter and more concrete: “Blessed are you who are poor.”
7 | Jesus heals a centurion’s servant and raises a widow’s son at Nain. A sinful woman weeps on his feet and anoints them: “Her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much.” || Jesus’s compassion for women and outsiders is a theme of Luke.
8 | Women who travel with and support Jesus are named. The parable of the sower. Jesus calms a storm, frees a man possessed by Legion, heals a bleeding woman and raises Jairus’s daughter. || Mary Magdalene, Joanna and Susanna funded Jesus’s mission “from their possessions”.
9 | The Twelve are sent out; five thousand are fed; Peter confesses Jesus as “the Christ of God”; the transfiguration. Jesus “intently set his face to go to Jerusalem”. || 9:51 begins Luke’s long journey section (9:51–19:27), with much material found only in Luke.
10 | Seventy (or seventy-two) are sent out. The parable of the Good Samaritan answers, “Who is my neighbour?” Martha is busy serving while Mary sits at Jesus’s feet. || Jesus turns the question from “Who is my neighbour?” to “which of these three do you think seemed to be a neighbor”
11 | Jesus teaches the Lord’s Prayer and urges persistent asking: “How much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!” Woes on Pharisees and lawyers. || “if I by God’s finger cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come to you.”
12 | Beware of hypocrisy. The parable of the rich fool. “Consider the ravens… consider the lilies.” Be ready like servants waiting for their master. || “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”
13 | Repent, or likewise perish. Jesus heals a bent-over woman on the Sabbath. The mustard seed and yeast. The narrow door. Jesus laments over Jerusalem. || People will come from east and west to the Kingdom: “there are some who are last who will be first”
14 | At a dinner Jesus teaches humility and inviting the poor, the crippled, the lame and the blind. The parable of the great banquet; counting the cost of discipleship. || Hospitality to those who cannot repay is a picture of God’s Kingdom.
15 | Three parables of lost things: a lost sheep, a lost coin, and a lost son whose father runs to welcome him home, while the elder brother refuses to join the celebration. || Found only in Luke. Told to grumbling Pharisees, it ends with an open question to the elder brother, and to the reader.
16 | The parable of the shrewd manager, and of the rich man and Lazarus, the poor man at his gate. || “You can’t serve both God and Mammon.” The rich man is unnamed; the poor man has a name.
17 | Forgive repeatedly; faith like a mustard seed. Ten lepers are healed and only one, a Samaritan, returns to give thanks. “God’s Kingdom is within you.” || The phrase can mean “within you” or “among you”.
18 | The persistent widow; the Pharisee and the tax collector at prayer: “God, be merciful to me, a sinner!” Jesus blesses children and heals a blind beggar near Jericho. || The tax collector’s prayer became the basis of the Jesus Prayer in Eastern Christianity.
19 | Zacchaeus, a rich tax collector, climbs a tree to see Jesus and welcomes him: “Today, salvation has come to this house.” Jesus weeps over Jerusalem and clears the temple. || “The Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost” sums up Luke’s Gospel.
20 | Leaders question Jesus’s authority. The parable of the wicked tenants. Questions about taxes and the resurrection: God is “not the God of the dead, but of the living.” || Jesus warns against scribes who devour widows’ houses.
21 | A poor widow gives two small coins, more than all the rich. Jesus foretells the temple’s destruction and Jerusalem surrounded by armies. || Luke makes the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70 especially clear.
22 | Judas agrees to betray Jesus. At the Last Supper Jesus says, “Do this in memory of me,” and that the greatest must serve. He prays on the Mount of Olives, sweating like drops of blood, is arrested; Peter denies him, and Jesus looks at Peter. || “This cup is the new covenant in my blood.” Jesus heals the ear of the high priest’s servant.
23 | Jesus is tried before Pilate and Herod. On the cross he prays, “Father, forgive them, for they don’t know what they are doing,” promises a thief, “Today you will be with me in Paradise,” and dies saying, “Father, into your hands I commit my spirit.” || Three sayings found only in Luke. Pilate declares Jesus innocent three times.
24 | Women find the tomb empty. On the road to Emmaus the risen Jesus walks with two disciples, explains the Scriptures, and is recognized in the breaking of bread. He appears to the eleven, eats fish, and is taken up into heaven. || “Weren’t our hearts burning within us?” Jesus shows that Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms speak of him. Acts 1 continues the story.
