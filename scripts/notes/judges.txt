# sourced
1 | After Joshua’s death the tribes fight on, but many fail to drive out the Canaanites. || Judah took Jerusalem, or perhaps an area outside it, but could not keep it; it stayed outside Israel’s control until David [[tbd:010:Judges, Book of]] [[bible:Judges 1:21]]. Ephraim and Manasseh succeeded only when they attacked together [[tosn:Judges 1:22]].
2 | The angel of Yahweh rebukes Israel. A new generation forgets God, and a cycle begins: they serve idols, are oppressed, cry out, and God raises up a judge to rescue them. || A new generation that had not seen God’s saving power falls into a cycle of rebellion and partial restoration, which the rest of the book shows [[tosn:Judges 2:10-19]]. The judges were remembered mainly for their military victories [[tosn:Judges 2:16-19]] [[bible:Judges 2:16]].
3 | Othniel, Ehud the left-handed, and Shamgar rescue Israel. Ehud kills the fat king of Moab with a hidden sword. || Ehud was “restricted in the right hand”, which let him hide his weapon on his right hip [[tosn:Judges 3:15]]. The king’s servants hesitate at the door, and Ehud escapes [[tosn:Judges 3:24-25]].
4 | Deborah, a prophet and judge, sends Barak against the Canaanite general Sisera, and Jael kills Sisera with a tent peg. || Deborah was a prophet who also served as judge, and she is the main character of the story [[tosn:Judges 4:4-7]]. God often chooses leaders people would not expect [[bsn:Judges 3:7-5:31]]. Hebrews names Barak among the faithful [[tosn:Judges 4:6]] [[bible:Hebrews 11:32]].
5 | The Song of Deborah celebrates the victory. || The victory song credited to Deborah is one of the oldest Hebrew poems [[tosn:Judges 5:1-31]]. Heavy rain would have turned the ground to mud, making Sisera’s chariots a burden [[tosn:Judges 5:4-5]].
6 | Midianite raiders impoverish Israel. The angel of Yahweh calls Gideon, who tears down his father’s Baal altar and asks for signs with a fleece. || “Mighty man of valour” can also mean “strong warrior” or “rich man” [[tosn:Judges 6:12]]. Like other prophets and judges, Gideon doubts God’s presence and his own ability [[tosn:Judges 6:13-15]].
7 | God reduces Gideon’s army to three hundred, and they defeat Midian with trumpets, jars and torches. || God cuts the army so that Israel cannot boast, “My own hand has saved me” [[bible:Judges 7:2]]. Paul makes a similar point: the power belongs to God, not to us [[bible:2 Corinthians 4:7]].
8 | Gideon pursues the Midianite kings, refuses to be king, but makes an ephod that becomes an idol. || Gideon says, “Yahweh shall rule over you” [[bible:Judges 8:23]], yet he becomes as focused on revenge as on victory [[tosn:Judges 8:4-12]], and his son Abimelech later seizes power [[bible:Judges 8:31]] [[bible:Judges 9:1-6]].
9 | Gideon’s son Abimelech murders his brothers and makes himself king; Jotham’s fable of the trees warns against him. He dies when a woman drops a millstone on him. || Jotham uses a fable, in which plants act like people, to expose Abimelech [[tosn:Judges 9:7-21]], and the civil war that follows fulfils his curse [[tosn:Judges 9:22-57]].
10 | The minor judges Tola and Jair; Israel turns to many gods and cries out, and God is “grieved for the misery of Israel”. || After Gideon the phrase “The land had rest forty years” no longer appears [[tosn:Judges 10:1-5]]. When Israel puts away its foreign gods, the LORD is “grieved for the misery of Israel” [[bible:Judges 10:16]].
11 | Jephthah, an outcast, leads Israel against Ammon and makes a rash vow that costs his daughter. || The LORD never needs bargaining for his favour, and Jephthah’s vow risked a human sacrifice, which Israel’s law forbids [[tosn:Judges 11:31]]. The law allowed release from unwise vows and a payment instead, but Jephthah seems not to have known it [[tosn:Judges 11:35]].
12 | Ephraim fights Jephthah; men who say “Sibboleth” instead of “Shibboleth” are killed. Three minor judges follow. || Ephraim feels left out again, and Jephthah, unlike Gideon, answers with civil war [[tosn:Judges 12:1-6]]. “Shibboleth” is still used for a test word that exposes outsiders [[tosn:Judges 12:6]].
13 | An angel announces to a childless couple that their son Samson will be a Nazirite who will begin to rescue Israel from the Philistines. || God gives a son to a woman who could not have children, as he had done for Sarah, Rebekah and Rachel [[bsn:Judges 13:1-16:31]]. Samson is to be a Nazirite from birth [[bible:Judges 13:5]].
14 | Samson marries a Philistine woman, kills a lion, poses a riddle, and kills thirty men when his wife betrays it. || Marrying outside the covenant was a serious sign of unfaithfulness, but God can work even through human mistakes [[tosn:Judges 14:2]]. The Spirit first comes on Samson when a lion attacks him [[tosn:Judges 14:5-6]].
15 | Samson burns the Philistines’ fields with foxes and kills a thousand men with a donkey’s jawbone. || Samson’s couplet plays on lekhi, which means both “jawbone” and the place Lehi [[tosn:Judges 15:16]]. Afraid of dying of thirst, he prays, and God splits the hollow place to give water [[tosn:Judges 15:18]] [[bible:Judges 15:19]].
16 | Delilah discovers Samson’s secret, his hair is cut, and he is blinded. In Dagon’s temple he prays and pulls down the pillars. || Samson abandoned his calling and lost his gifts, regaining strength only when he prayed again at the end [[tosn:Judges 16:1-31]] [[bible:Judges 16:28]]. Hebrews still lists him among the faithful [[bible:Hebrews 11:32]].
17 | Micah makes an idol and hires a wandering Levite as his priest. || The two closing stories show that inspired but unstructured leadership could not last; “there was no king in Israel” appears four times [[tosn:Judges 17:1-21:25]] [[bible:Judges 17:6]].
18 | The tribe of Dan steals Micah’s idol and priest, conquers the peaceful town of Laish and sets up their own shrine. || The wandering Levite is named as Jonathan, a descendant of Moses through Gershom [[tosn:Judges 18:30]].
19 | A Levite’s concubine is abused and killed at Gibeah in Benjamin, and he sends her body in pieces to the tribes. || The old man’s urgency and the men’s demand recall Sodom [[tosn:Judges 19:20]] [[tosn:Judges 19:22]] [[bible:Genesis 19:4-5]].
20 | The tribes go to war against Benjamin, and Benjamin is nearly wiped out. || Israel holds the whole town responsible, since Gibeah did not punish the guilty [[tosn:Judges 20:12-13]]. Hosea later recalls “the days of Gibeah” as a picture of deep corruption [[bible:Hosea 9:9]].
21 | To keep Benjamin alive, the tribes seize wives for its survivors. || A foolish vow causes trouble for the second time in the book [[tosn:Judges 21:1]]. The closing words explain the disorder of the age; later God makes a covenant with King David [[tosn:Judges 21:25]] [[bible:Judges 21:25]].
