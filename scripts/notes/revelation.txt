1 | John, exiled on Patmos, sees the risen Christ among seven lampstands, with eyes like fire and a face like the sun: “Don’t be afraid. I am the first and the last, and the Living one.” || “Revelation” translates apokalypsis, “unveiling”. Christ holds “the keys of Death and of Hades”.
2 | Letters to the churches of Ephesus (you have left your first love), Smyrna (be faithful to death), Pergamum and Thyatira. || Each letter has praise, rebuke and a promise “to him who overcomes”. The cities were real places in what is now Turkey.
3 | Letters to Sardis, Philadelphia and lukewarm Laodicea: “Behold, I stand at the door and knock.” || Laodicea’s water, piped in lukewarm, may lie behind the image.
4 | A door opens in heaven: John sees God’s throne with a rainbow, twenty-four elders and four living creatures singing, “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty.” || The throne room draws on Isaiah 6 and Ezekiel 1. Before any judgement, God is on the throne.
5 | A scroll sealed with seven seals: no one can open it until the Lion of Judah appears, and he is a Lamb that had been slain. All creation sings, “Worthy is the Lamb.” || The interpretive key to the book: God’s power is revealed in the sacrificed Lamb.
6 | The Lamb opens six seals: four horsemen (conquest, war, famine, death), the martyrs’ cry “How long?”, and a great earthquake. || The four horsemen draw on Zechariah 1 and 6.
7 | 144,000 from the tribes of Israel are sealed, then John sees a great multitude no one can count, from every nation, before the throne. God will wipe away every tear. || John hears a number and sees a multitude, perhaps the same people seen two ways.
8 | The seventh seal: silence in heaven, prayers rising like incense, then seven trumpets begin with plagues on the earth. || The trumpet plagues echo the plagues of Egypt.
9 | The fifth and sixth trumpets: locusts from the abyss and a vast army; yet people do not repent. || Judgements in Revelation aim at repentance.
10 | A mighty angel with a little scroll; John eats it, sweet in his mouth but bitter in his stomach. || Echoes Ezekiel 3: the message is both sweet and costly.
11 | Two witnesses prophesy, are killed, and raised. The seventh trumpet: “The kingdom of the world has become the Kingdom of our Lord, and of his Christ. He will reign forever and ever.” || Handel’s “Hallelujah Chorus” sets 11:15.
12 | A woman clothed with the sun gives birth to a son; a great dragon tries to devour him. Michael defeats the dragon, Satan, who is thrown down. Believers overcome “because of the Lamb’s blood”. || A cosmic retelling of Christ’s birth and victory, echoing Genesis 3:15.
13 | A beast rises from the sea, given authority by the dragon, and a second beast from the land makes people worship it. Its number is 666. || 666 is widely thought to spell “Nero Caesar” in Hebrew letters; some manuscripts read 616, which also fits.
14 | The Lamb stands on Mount Zion with the 144,000. Angels proclaim the eternal gospel and the fall of Babylon; a harvest of the earth. || “Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord.”
15 | The victors sing the song of Moses and the Lamb; seven angels receive bowls of God’s wrath. || A new exodus song beside a sea of glass.
16 | The seven bowls are poured out: sores, blood, scorching heat, darkness; the kings gather at Armageddon. || “Armageddon” likely means “mountain of Megiddo”, a famous battlefield.
17 | A woman, “Babylon the great”, rides a scarlet beast, drunk with the blood of the saints. The Lamb will overcome, “for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings.” || The seven hills (17:9) suggest Rome; Babylon stands for every proud empire.
18 | Babylon falls; kings and merchants lament her lost luxury. “Come out of her, my people.” || Draws on the laments over Tyre (Ezekiel 27) and Babylon (Jeremiah 51).
19 | Heaven sings “Hallelujah!” for the marriage supper of the Lamb. Christ rides a white horse, called Faithful and True and the Word of God, and defeats the beast. || The only place in the New Testament where “Hallelujah” appears.
20 | Satan is bound for a thousand years; the martyrs reign with Christ. Satan is released, defeated, and the dead are judged before a great white throne. || The millennium is interpreted as a literal future reign (premillennial), the church age (amillennial), or a golden age before Christ’s return (postmillennial).
21 | “I saw a new heaven and a new earth.” The holy city, new Jerusalem, comes down from God: “God himself will be with them… he will wipe away every tear… death will be no more.” “Behold, I am making all things new.” || The city is a perfect cube, like the Most Holy Place: the whole city is God’s dwelling.
22 | The river of the water of life flows from the throne, and the tree of life bears fruit for the healing of the nations. “Behold, I come quickly.” “Come, Lord Jesus.” || The Bible ends where it began: a garden, a river and the tree of life, now opened to all. The last prayer is the early Christian Maranatha.
