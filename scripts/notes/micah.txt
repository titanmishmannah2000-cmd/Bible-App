1 | God comes out of his place to judge Samaria and Jerusalem for their idols. Micah laments, going barefoot. || Moresheth was a fortress town near Gath in Judah’s low hills [[tosn:Micah 1:1]]. In “the prophet’s lament” (1:8–16) an army threatens twelve cities, with a wordplay on each name except Gath [[tbd:002:Book of Micah]].
2 | Woe to those who lie awake planning to seize fields and houses. Yet God will gather the remnant like sheep. || A woe on wealthy men who plot at night to take farmers’ houses and fields; their own land will be taken [[tbd:002:Book of Micah]] [[tosn:Micah 2:4]].
3 | Against rulers who “hate the good and love the evil”, and prophets who preach for pay. Zion will be ploughed like a field. || Mount Zion would become a wilderness [[tosn:Micah 3:12]]. A century later elders quoted this verse at Jeremiah’s trial, and he was spared [[bible:Jeremiah 26:18-19]] [[bible:Jeremiah 26:24]].
4 | In the last days the nations will stream to God’s mountain and beat their swords into ploughshares; “they will sit every man under his vine and under his fig tree” || Verses 1–3 closely match Isaiah 2:2–4 [[bible:Isaiah 2:2-4]]. Each under his own vine and fig tree means life without enemies, as in Solomon’s time [[tosn:Micah 4:4]].
5 | “But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, being small among the clans of Judah, out of you one will come out to me that is to be ruler in Israel.” He will shepherd his flock and be their peace. || Ephrathah is the old name of Bethlehem, David’s birthplace; a ruler greater than David would come from there [[tosn:Micah 5:2]], quoted in Matthew 2:5–6 [[bible:Matthew 2:5-6]].
6 | God brings a case against his people: “What have I done to you?” “He has shown you, O man, what is good. What does Yahweh require of you, but to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God?” || Justice (mishpat) means treating others fairly; mercy (khesed) is the loyal love that marks God’s own character; walking humbly means reverent fear of God [[tosn:Micah 6:8]]. For Israel this is Micah’s answer to sin [[tbd:002:Book of Micah]].
7 | Micah grieves over a society without honest people, where “a man’s enemies are the men of his own house.” Yet: “Who is a God like you, who pardons iniquity?… You will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea.” || Jesus uses the words of 7:6 about divided households [[tosn:Micah 7:5–6]] [[bible:Matthew 10:35-36]]. “Who is a God like you” plays on Micah’s name, “Who is like the Lord?” [[tosn:Micah 7:18–20]].
