# sourced
1 | Old King David is frail. His son Adonijah declares himself king, but Nathan and Bathsheba persuade David to have Solomon anointed. || 1 Kings opens with the LORD’s promise to David, and Solomon is the rightful, God-chosen heir [[tosn:1 Kings 1:1–2:12]]. Adonijah was David’s fourth son and expected the throne because his older brothers had died [[tosn:1 Kings 1:5–6]].
2 | David charges Solomon to walk in God’s ways, then dies. Solomon secures his throne by removing Adonijah, Joab and Shimei. || David’s charge, “You be strong therefore, and show yourself a man”, echoes the words to Joshua [[tosn:1 Kings 2:2]] [[bible:Joshua 1:6-7]].
3 | God appears to Solomon in a dream and offers him anything; Solomon asks for wisdom to rule well. He judges between two mothers claiming one baby. || Solomon humbly admits he is inexperienced, “a little child” [[tosn:1 Kings 3:7–9]], and asks for “an understanding heart to judge your people” [[bible:1 Kings 3:9]]; God adds blessings he did not ask for [[tosn:1 Kings 3:4–15]].
4 | Solomon’s officials and the prosperity of his kingdom. His wisdom is famous: three thousand proverbs and a thousand and five songs. || Israel “numerous as the sand” and Solomon’s wide rule show God’s promises to Abraham coming true [[tosn:1 Kings 4:20–21]] [[bible:Genesis 22:17]].
5 | Solomon partners with Hiram of Tyre for cedar and forced labour to build the temple. || Forced labour was common in the ancient Near East; foreigners living in Israel did most of it [[tosn:1 Kings 5:13–18]] [[tosn:1 Kings 5:13]]. Complaints about the heavy burden later split the kingdom [[bible:1 Kings 12:4]].
6 | Solomon builds the temple in seven years, with cedar walls covered in gold and carved cherubim. || Solomon’s fourth year was about 967 BC. Taken as calendar years, the 480 years date the Exodus to about 1446 BC; others read the number as symbolic [[tosn:1 Kings 6:1]]. Like the tabernacle, the temple was where God met his people [[tosn:1 Kings 6:2]].
7 | Solomon’s palace takes thirteen years. The bronze pillars, the great Sea and the temple furnishings are made. || The palace took almost twice as long as the temple, thirteen years against seven [[tosn:1 Kings 7:1–12]].
8 | The ark is brought into the temple, and the glory-cloud fills it. Solomon prays: “Heaven and the heaven of heavens can’t contain you; how much less this house!” || “Heaven and the heaven of heavens can’t contain you” [[bible:1 Kings 8:27]]. Solomon asks God to hear even the foreigner who prays toward the temple [[bible:1 Kings 8:41-43]], and praises God for keeping his promises [[tosn:1 Kings 8:12–21]].
9 | God answers Solomon with promise and warning: if Israel turns away, the temple will become a ruin. || If Israel turned away, the covenant curses would fall, including the destruction of the temple [[bsn:1 Kings 9:1–9]]. Kings was written in exile to answer why that had happened [[tbd:011:Kings, Books of First and Second]].
10 | The Queen of Sheba visits and is amazed at Solomon’s wisdom. His wealth is described. || The queen admired Solomon’s wisdom and saw that the LORD was behind him [[tosn:1 Kings 10:6–9]]. Jesus praised her for seeking the truth [[tosn:1 Kings 10:3–5]] [[bible:Matthew 12:42]]. Solomon’s horses, wives and wealth went against God’s rules for kings in Deuteronomy 17 [[bsn:1 Kings 9:10–11:43]].
11 | Solomon’s many foreign wives turn his heart to other gods. God raises up enemies and promises ten tribes to Jeroboam. || Despite his wisdom, Solomon broke the law of Moses by gathering wealth and marrying many foreign wives [[tosn:1 Kings 11:1–3]]. “His wives turned away his heart after other gods” [[bible:1 Kings 11:4]].
12 | Solomon’s son Rehoboam refuses to lighten the people’s burden, and the ten northern tribes make Jeroboam king. Jeroboam sets up golden calves at Bethel and Dan. || Rehoboam ruled from 931 to 913 BC [[tosn:1 Kings 12:1]]. Bethel and Dan had long religious traditions [[tosn:1 Kings 12:29–30]]. Jeroboam’s words over the calves repeat Aaron’s at Sinai [[bible:1 Kings 12:28]] [[bible:Exodus 32:4]].
13 | A man of God from Judah condemns Jeroboam’s altar but is deceived by an old prophet and killed by a lion. || The story shows the authority of God’s word: the prophecy even names Josiah, who fulfils it much later [[tosn:1 Kings 13:1–10]] [[bible:2 Kings 23:15-16]]. The old prophet lied about an angel’s message [[tosn:1 Kings 13:11–19]].
14 | Jeroboam’s son falls ill, and the prophet Ahijah announces judgement on his house. Egypt’s Pharaoh Shishak raids Jerusalem. || Shishak (Shoshenq I) ruled Egypt from 945 to 924 BC and founded its 22nd dynasty [[tosn:1 Kings 14:25]].
15 | Kings Abijam and Asa of Judah, and Nadab and Baasha of Israel. Asa removes idols. || Every king of Judah is compared with David [[bsn:1 Kings 15:1–22:53]]. Even when Abijam was unfaithful, God kept his promise to keep David’s descendants on the throne [[tosn:1 Kings 15:3–5]].
16 | A rapid series of northern kings, then Omri and his son Ahab, who marries Jezebel and worships Baal. || Omri gets only eight verses, though politically he was one of the greatest northern kings, because the writer’s concerns are theological [[tbd:011:Kings, Books of First and Second]]. His house brought the north’s dynastic instability to an end for four generations [[tbd:011:Kings, Books of First and Second]]. Ahab did more evil than all before him [[bible:1 Kings 16:30]].
17 | Elijah announces a drought, is fed by ravens, and provides endless flour and oil for a widow in Zarephath, then raises her son. || Ironically, God sheltered Elijah at Zarephath, in Jezebel’s Baal-worshipping homeland [[tosn:1 Kings 17:9–11]]. Jesus cites this widow in Luke 4:25–26 [[bible:Luke 4:25-26]].
18 | On Mount Carmel Elijah challenges the prophets of Baal: “How long will you waver between the two sides?” God answers with fire, and rain returns. || Ahab, not Elijah, was the real “troubler”, since he broke the covenant and promoted Baal [[tosn:1 Kings 18:16–18]]. “Waver” means to limp from one foot to the other [[tosn:1 Kings 18:21]]. James cites Elijah’s prayer for drought and rain [[bible:James 5:17-18]].
19 | Fleeing Jezebel, Elijah collapses in despair. At Horeb God comes not in wind, earthquake or fire, but in “a still small voice”. Elijah calls Elisha. || Elijah travels to Mount Sinai (Horeb), about 200 miles; his forty days may echo Moses’ forty days there [[tosn:1 Kings 19:8]]. God fed him through an angel [[bsn:1 Kings 15:1–22:53]].
20 | Ahab defeats the Arameans twice but spares their king against God’s will. || The Arameans thought Israel’s God had power only in the hills, which gave the LORD a chance to show who he is [[tosn:1 Kings 20:23]] [[bible:1 Kings 20:28]].
21 | Ahab and Jezebel have Naboth killed to take his vineyard. Elijah confronts Ahab, who humbles himself. || The law required inherited land to stay in the family, so Naboth refused [[tosn:1 Kings 21:2–3]] [[bible:Leviticus 25:23]]. Jezebel acted like a Canaanite ruler who did as he pleased [[tosn:1 Kings 21:7]].
22 | Ahab and Jehoshaphat ask about war. Four hundred prophets say yes; Micaiah alone tells the truth. Ahab dies in battle. || Kings often sought God’s will before battle [[tosn:1 Kings 22:5–9]]. Micaiah promises to speak only the LORD’s message, not agree with the court prophets [[tosn:1 Kings 22:13–14]]; Ahab hates him “for he does not prophesy good concerning me” [[bible:1 Kings 22:8]].
