1 | Jesus’s family tree from Abraham through David, then his birth: an angel tells Joseph that Mary’s child is from the Holy Spirit, to be named Jesus, “for it is he who shall save his people from their sins.” || The genealogy is arranged in three sets of fourteen and includes four women, among them foreigners. Matthew quotes Isaiah 7:14: Immanuel, “God with us”.
2 | Wise men from the east follow a star to worship the child in Bethlehem. King Herod tries to kill him; the family flees to Egypt, and later settles in Nazareth. || Jesus relives Israel’s story: out of Egypt (Hosea 11:1). Herod died in 4 BC, so Jesus was born a little before that.
3 | John the Baptizer preaches in the wilderness: “Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!” Jesus is baptized, the Spirit descends like a dove, and a voice says, “This is my beloved Son.” || The Father, Son and Spirit all appear together. The voice combines Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1.
4 | Jesus is tempted in the wilderness for forty days and answers the devil with Scripture. He begins preaching in Galilee and calls fishermen: “Come after me, and I will make you fishers for men.” || All three answers come from Deuteronomy 6–8: Jesus succeeds where Israel failed in the wilderness.
5 | The Sermon on the Mount begins: the Beatitudes (“Blessed are the poor in spirit”), salt and light, and Jesus’s teaching that goes beyond the law: anger, lust, oaths, revenge, and “Love your enemies.” || Jesus says he came not to abolish the law but to fulfil it. The repeated “You have heard… but I tell you” shows his authority.
6 | Give, pray and fast in secret, not for show. The Lord’s Prayer. Store up treasure in heaven. “Don’t be anxious… seek first God’s Kingdom.” || The Lord’s Prayer is at the centre of the sermon. The doxology “For yours is the Kingdom…” is missing from the oldest manuscripts.
7 | Don’t judge. Ask, seek, knock. The Golden Rule. The narrow gate. A tree is known by its fruit. The wise man builds his house on the rock. || The crowds are amazed because he teaches with authority, not like the scribes.
8 | Jesus heals a leper, a centurion’s servant and Peter’s mother-in-law, calms a storm, and frees two men from demons. || The centurion’s faith leads Jesus to say many will come from east and west to the feast with Abraham.
9 | Jesus heals a paralysed man and forgives his sins, calls Matthew the tax collector, raises a girl, and heals a bleeding woman and two blind men. “The harvest indeed is plentiful, but the labourers are few.” || Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6: “I desire mercy, and not sacrifice.”
10 | Jesus sends out the twelve apostles to preach and heal, warning of persecution: “Don’t be afraid… you are of more value than many sparrows.” || The second great teaching block. A cup of cold water given to a disciple will be rewarded.
11 | John the Baptist in prison asks if Jesus is the one; Jesus points to his works. “Come to me, all you who labour and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest.” || Jesus says John is the Elijah who was to come. The invitation echoes Wisdom’s call.
12 | Conflicts over the Sabbath. The Pharisees say Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebul. Jesus speaks of the sign of Jonah and of his true family. || Matthew quotes Isaiah 42 about the gentle servant. “one greater than the temple is here”
13 | Parables of the Kingdom: the sower, the weeds, the mustard seed, the yeast, the hidden treasure, the pearl and the net. Jesus is rejected in Nazareth. || The third teaching block. A scribe trained for the Kingdom brings out treasures new and old.
14 | Herod beheads John the Baptist. Jesus feeds five thousand, walks on the water, and Peter steps out of the boat, then sinks. || “You are truly the Son of God!”: the disciples worship him in the boat.
15 | Jesus teaches that what defiles comes from the heart. A Canaanite woman’s great faith; healings; the feeding of four thousand. || Even “the dogs eat the crumbs”: the woman’s reply wins Jesus’s praise.
16 | Peter confesses, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” Jesus says he will build his church on this rock, then foretells his death and calls followers to take up their cross. || The meaning of “this rock” (Peter, his confession, or Christ) has been debated between Catholics and Protestants.
17 | Jesus is transfigured on a mountain with Moses and Elijah. He heals a boy and pays the temple tax with a coin from a fish’s mouth. || “Faith as a grain of mustard seed” can move mountains.
18 | Who is greatest? Become like a child. The lost sheep; how to correct a brother; forgive “seventy times seven”; the parable of the unforgiving servant. || The fourth teaching block, on life together in the community of disciples.
19 | Teaching on marriage and divorce; Jesus blesses children; a rich young man goes away sad. “With God all things are possible.” || “many will be last who are first”
20 | The parable of the workers in the vineyard, paid the same. Jesus foretells his death again, teaches that the greatest must serve, and heals two blind men. || The Son of Man came to serve and to give his life as a ransom for many.
21 | Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey to cries of “Hosanna to the son of David!”, clears the temple, curses a fig tree, and tells parables of two sons and the wicked tenants. || Matthew quotes Zechariah 9:9. The rejected stone becomes the cornerstone (Psalm 118).
22 | The parable of the wedding feast. Questions about taxes, the resurrection and the greatest commandment. Whose son is the Christ? || Love God and love neighbour: “The whole law and the prophets depend on these two commandments.”
23 | Seven woes against the scribes and Pharisees for hypocrisy. Jesus laments over Jerusalem: “How often I would have gathered your children together, even as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings” || Some of Jesus’s sharpest words, spoken as a Jewish prophet within Israel, not against the Jewish people.
24 | On the Mount of Olives Jesus foretells the temple’s destruction, wars and persecution, and the coming of the Son of Man. “Watch.” || The fifth teaching block begins (24–25). Scholars debate which parts refer to AD 70 and which to the end.
25 | Parables of the ten bridesmaids and the talents, and the judgement of the nations: “because you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me.” || 25:31–46 has shaped Christian care for the hungry, strangers, the sick and prisoners.
26 | A woman anoints Jesus; Judas agrees to betray him for thirty pieces of silver. At the Last Supper Jesus gives bread and wine. He prays in Gethsemane, “not what I desire, but what you desire” is arrested, tried, and Peter denies him. || “This is my blood of the new covenant, which is poured out for many for the remission of sins.”
27 | Judas hangs himself. Pilate washes his hands and hands Jesus over. He is crucified; darkness falls; the curtain tears; the earth shakes. Joseph of Arimathea buries him, and the tomb is guarded. || Only Matthew reports Pilate’s handwashing and the tombs opening. The line “His blood be on us and on our children” was tragically misused to justify antisemitism.
28 | On the first day of the week the women find the stone rolled away; the risen Jesus meets them. In Galilee he tells the eleven: “Go and make disciples of all nations… I am with you always, even to the end of the age.” || The Great Commission, with baptism in the name of the Father, Son and Holy Spirit. The Gospel ends as it began: God with us (1:23; 28:20).
