1 | “Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth; for your love is better than wine.” A young woman longs for her beloved, and they praise each other’s beauty. || The NLT rightly reads “song of songs” as a Hebrew superlative: the finest song [[tosn:Song of Solomon 1:1]]. In the Song, the woman often begins the relationship [[tosn:Song of Solomon 1:2–14]]. Jewish and Christian scholars long read it as an allegory, Christians of the love between Christ and the church [[tbd:019:Song of Solomon]].
2 | “I am a rose of Sharon.” Her beloved comes leaping over the hills: “Rise up, my love, my beautiful one, and come away,” for winter is past. || They are lovers and also friends [[tosn:Song of Solomon 2:1–2]]. The refrain urges “that you not stir up, nor awaken love, until it so desires” [[bible:Song of Solomon 2:7]], repeated three times [[tosn:Song of Solomon 8:4]].
3 | At night she searches the city for the one she loves, finds him and will not let him go. Solomon’s wedding procession arrives. || The poem shows a lover’s determination to overcome every obstacle [[tosn:Song of Solomon 3:2]].
4 | The bridegroom praises his bride’s beauty from head to toe: “You are all beautiful, my love. There is no spot in you.” || With this poem the marriage is complete [[tosn:Song of Solomon 4:1–5:1]]. It is the first of the book’s “descriptive poems”, which praise the beloved’s body [[tosn:Song of Solomon 4:1–5:1]]. Ephesians 5:27 also speaks of a bride “without spot” [[bible:Ephesians 5:27]].
5 | She hesitates to open the door, then he is gone; she searches again, describing him to the daughters of Jerusalem: “This is my beloved, and this is my friend.” || “I was asleep, but my heart was awake” describes a dream [[tosn:Song of Solomon 5:2]]. There is no guilt or shame in the pleasure of husband and wife [[tosn:Song of Solomon 5:1]].
6 | The beloved has gone down to his garden; she says, “I am my beloved’s, and my beloved is mine.” He praises her again. || The conversation shows the scene was a dream, not a real event [[tosn:Song of Solomon 6:1–3]]. He repeats his earlier praise almost word for word [[tosn:Song of Solomon 6:4–10]].
7 | He delights in her beauty; she invites him to the fields and vineyards. || Another descriptive poem, from her dancing feet up to her hair [[tosn:Song of Solomon 7:1–5]].
8 | “Love is strong as death… Many waters can’t quench love.” The song ends with longing and invitation. || Love is resolute and broken only by death; jealousy protects an exclusive bond, as between God and his people [[tosn:Song of Solomon 8:6]]. The WEB ends 8:6 with “a very flame of Yahweh” [[bible:Song of Solomon 8:6]]. Nothing in creation or money can stop love [[bsn:Song of Solomon 1:1–8:14]].
