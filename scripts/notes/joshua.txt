# sourced
1 | After Moses dies, God commissions Joshua to lead Israel into the land: “Be strong and courageous… for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go.” || God tells Joshua three times to be strong and courageous [[tosn:Joshua 1:6-9]]. Meditating on the law “day and night” [[bible:Joshua 1:8]] is the same picture as Psalm 1 [[bible:Psalm 1:2]]. The land belongs to God, and Israel enters it as his tenants [[tosn:Joshua 1:6-9]].
2 | Two spies go to Jericho and are hidden by Rahab, a prostitute, who confesses faith in Israel’s God. She hangs a scarlet cord from her window. || Rahab begins to declare her faith in the LORD [[tosn:Joshua 2:9-10]]. She appears in Jesus’s family tree [[bible:Matthew 1:5]] and is praised for her faith [[bible:Hebrews 11:31]] and her deeds [[bible:James 2:25]].
3 | The priests carry the ark into the Jordan, the river stops flowing, and Israel crosses on dry ground. || The ark stood for God’s presence and his throne, and priests carried it ahead of the people [[tosn:Joshua 3:3]]. Joshua says God dried the Jordan as he did the Red Sea [[bible:Joshua 4:23]].
4 | Twelve stones from the riverbed are set up at Gilgal as a memorial for future children. || The twelve stones from the riverbed were to remind Israel to teach their children what God had done [[bsn:Joshua 3:1-5:12]] [[bible:Joshua 4:6-7]].
5 | The new generation is circumcised, keeps Passover, and the manna stops. Joshua meets the commander of Yahweh’s army. || “Take off your sandals” repeats what God told Moses at the burning bush, and Joshua recognised the link [[tosn:Joshua 5:15]] [[tbd:010:Joshua, Book of]].
6 | Israel marches around Jericho for seven days; on the seventh the priests blow trumpets, the people shout, and the walls fall. Rahab’s family is saved. || Marching seven times on the seventh day showed that this was God’s battle [[tosn:Joshua 6:15]]; the LORD gives the victory [[nabre:joshua/6]]. Hebrews cites the fall of the walls as an act of faith [[bible:Hebrews 11:30]]. Joshua’s curse on rebuilding Jericho was fulfilled under King Ahab [[nabre:joshua/6]] [[tbd:010:Jericho]].
7 | Israel is defeated at Ai because Achan secretly took forbidden plunder. He is found and executed. || Achan’s hidden sin brings defeat on all Israel [[bible:Joshua 7:11-12]]. Joshua pleads for the honour of God’s “great name”, as Moses had [[tosn:Joshua 7:8-9]] [[bible:Exodus 32:11-12]].
8 | Using an ambush Israel takes Ai, and Joshua reads the whole law at Mount Ebal as Moses commanded. || At Mount Ebal Joshua does what Moses commanded: an altar, stones plastered and written with the law, and the blessings and curses read aloud [[tosn:Joshua 8:32]] [[tosn:Joshua 8:34]] [[bible:Deuteronomy 27:2-8]].
9 | The Gibeonites trick Israel into a peace treaty by pretending to come from far away. Israel keeps its oath. || Israel’s leaders “didn’t ask counsel from Yahweh’s mouth” [[bible:Joshua 9:14]]. The oath to the Gibeonites still mattered in David’s day [[bible:2 Samuel 21:1-2]].
10 | Joshua defends Gibeon against five kings, and the sun stands still until the battle is won. || The sun and moon, worshipped as gods in Canaan, were powerless against the LORD [[tosn:Joshua 10:12]]. The words come from the Book of Jashar, an ancient source that has not survived [[tosn:Joshua 10:13]]. Not every miracle can be fully understood, including how it happened [[tosn:Joshua 10:13]].
11 | Joshua defeats a northern coalition led by Jabin of Hazor. The land has rest from war. || Hazor was the largest and most important inland city of Canaan [[tosn:Joshua 11:1-3]]. Excavations show that Hazor was destroyed by fire in this period [[tosn:Joshua 11:11]].
12 | A list of the thirty-one kings Moses and Joshua defeated. || The list sums up Israel’s victories on both sides of the Jordan before the land is divided [[tosn:Joshua 12:1-24]].
13 | Much land remains to be taken. The territory east of the Jordan is described for Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh. || Israel did not conquer all of Canaan in Joshua’s lifetime; the most populated valleys and plains took generations [[tosn:Joshua 13:1-7]] [[bible:Joshua 13:1]]. The NABRE sees Israel’s rise in the land as slow and piecemeal, as Judges 1 shows [[nabre:joshua/0]].
14 | Land is divided by lot. Caleb, now eighty-five, asks for the hill country God promised him. || Only Caleb and Joshua had trusted that Israel could take the land, so God promised it to them [[tosn:Joshua 14:6]] [[bible:Numbers 14:24]]. Caleb “wholly followed Yahweh” [[bible:Joshua 14:14]].
15 | The boundaries and towns of Judah, including Jerusalem, which they could not conquer. || Neither Judah nor Benjamin controlled Jerusalem [[tosn:Joshua 15:8]] [[bible:Joshua 15:63]]; it was taken only by David [[bible:2 Samuel 5:6-7]].
16 | The territory of Ephraim, Joseph’s son. || Gezer apparently did not come into Israel’s hands until King Solomon’s time [[tosn:Joshua 16:3]] [[bible:1 Kings 9:16]].
17 | The territory of Manasseh, and land for Zelophehad’s daughters as promised. || Ephraim and Manasseh together already had more land than any other tribe [[tosn:Joshua 17:14]], and Joshua tells them to clear the forested hills [[bible:Joshua 17:18]].
18 | The tabernacle is set up at Shiloh, and the remaining land is surveyed; Benjamin’s territory is described. || Joshua set up the Tent of Meeting at Shiloh, making it Israel’s religious and political centre [[tosn:Joshua 18:1]]; the ark is still there in Samuel’s day [[bible:1 Samuel 4:3]].
19 | Territories for Simeon, Zebulun, Issachar, Asher, Naphtali and Dan, and a town for Joshua. || Simeon’s towns lay within Judah’s land, and most of Simeon later became part of Judah [[tosn:Joshua 19:2-9]], in line with Jacob’s words, “scatter them in Israel” [[tosn:Joshua 19:1]] [[bible:Genesis 49:7]].
20 | Six cities of refuge are named. || These verses put into practice the law of the cities of refuge in Numbers 35 [[tosn:Joshua 20:1-6]]. Foreigners living in Israel were to receive the same justice [[tosn:Joshua 20:9]].
21 | Forty-eight towns are given to the Levites. “Nothing failed of any good thing which Yahweh had spoken” || The Levites received towns throughout the tribes, which helped them teach God’s law everywhere [[tosn:Joshua 21:2-3]]. The chapter ends: “Nothing failed of any good thing which Yahweh had spoken” [[bible:Joshua 21:45]].
22 | The eastern tribes go home and build an altar by the Jordan, nearly causing civil war until they explain it is a witness, not a rival altar. || Joshua sends the eastern tribes home, urging them to love God with all their heart [[tosn:Joshua 22:5]] [[bible:Deuteronomy 6:5]]. Their altar was meant as a witness, not for sacrifice [[bible:Joshua 22:26-27]].
23 | Old Joshua urges Israel’s leaders to stay faithful and not to mix with the remaining nations. || God left some peoples in the land to test Israel’s faithfulness [[tosn:Joshua 23:4-5]] [[bible:Judges 2:20-23]]. When Israel turned away, he did not drive them out [[tosn:Joshua 23:15-16]].
24 | At Shechem Joshua retells Israel’s story and renews the covenant: “As for me and my house, we will serve Yahweh.” He dies, and Joseph’s bones are buried. || The covenant at Shechem follows the form of an ancient treaty between a great king and a weaker people [[tosn:Joshua 24:1-27]]. Israel’s faith begins with what God has done: “I took… I gave… I sent… I brought” [[tosn:Joshua 24:3-13]]. Joshua’s name means “the LORD saves”, the same name as Jesus [[nabre:numbers/13]].
