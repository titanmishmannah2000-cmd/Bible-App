1 | A devastating plague of locusts strips the land. Joel calls everyone to lament, fast and cry to God, for the day of Yahweh is near. || The four kinds of locusts may be stages of growth, or picture four empires [[tosn:Joel 1:4]]. The plague stopped the temple offerings, so even the priests mourned [[tosn:Joel 1:9]] [[tbd:002:Book of Joel]].
2 | An army like locusts comes on the day of Yahweh. “Tear your heart, and not your garments, and turn to Yahweh.” God promises to restore “the years that the swarming locust has eaten” and to pour out his Spirit on all flesh. || Scholars read 2:1–11 as the same plague, a future plague, or an invading army pictured as locusts [[tosn:Joel 2:1–11]]. God would pour out his Spirit on everyone, whatever their age or status [[tbd:002:Book of Joel]]; Peter explains Pentecost with these words [[bsn:Joel 2:28–3:21]] [[bible:Acts 2:16-21]].
3 | God gathers the nations for judgement in the valley of Jehoshaphat, “the valley of decision”; Judah will be blessed and the mountains will drip with sweet wine. || “Beat your plowshares into swords” deliberately reverses the peace of Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3 [[tosn:Joel 3:10]]. The book ends with the opposite of its beginning: a secure and fruitful Judah [[tosn:Joel 3:20–21]].
