# sourced
1 | Job is a good man with a large family and great wealth. In heaven the Accuser says Job only serves God because he is blessed. In one day Job loses his children and possessions, yet says, “Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away. Blessed be Yahweh’s name.” || Satan asks, “Does Job fear God for nothing?” [[bible:Job 1:9]], suggesting Job’s faith depends on his wealth [[tosn:Job 1:9–11]]. None of the characters ever learn of this council in heaven [[tosn:Job 1:1–2:13]].
2 | Job is struck with painful sores. His wife tells him to curse God and die. Three friends come and sit with him in silence for seven days. || Seven days and nights was the usual time of grief [[tosn:Job 2:13]]. By staying silent the friends showed respect and waited for Job to speak first [[bsn:Job 2:11–3:26]].
3 | Job breaks his silence and curses the day he was born, wishing he had never lived. || Job curses his birthday but not God [[tosn:Job 3:1–10]]. His outburst did not mean his integrity failed; Elijah and Jeremiah used similar language [[tosn:Job 3:1–26]] [[bible:Jeremiah 20:14-18]].
4 | Eliphaz gently suggests that the innocent never perish: Job must have sinned. He describes a spirit whispering to him at night. || Eliphaz must have known that innocent people sometimes do die [[tosn:Job 4:7]]. He believed trouble comes to foolish people as a lesson [[bsn:Job 4:1–31:40]].
5 | Eliphaz urges Job to seek God and accept his discipline. || Paul quotes the idea that God “takes the wise in their own craftiness” [[tosn:Job 5:13]] [[bible:1 Corinthians 3:19]]. Many things the friends said were true, but they applied them wrongly to Job [[bsn:Job 4:1–31:40]].
6 | Job says his grief outweighs the sand of the sea and his friends have failed him like dried-up streams. || Job and his friends may have shared a covenant of loyalty, which made their failure worse [[tosn:Job 6:14–27]]: “To him who is ready to faint, kindness should be shown from his friend” [[bible:Job 6:14]].
7 | Job laments the hard, brief life of man and asks God why he will not leave him alone. || “What is man, that you should magnify him” [[bible:Job 7:17]] echoes Psalm 8:4 [[bible:Psalm 8:4]]. Job says life is “but a breath” [[tosn:Job 7:7]].
8 | Bildad insists God does not twist justice: if Job is pure, God will restore him. || Bildad thought the death of Job’s children showed they had sinned [[tosn:Job 8:3–4]].
9 | Job agrees God is just, but asks how a man can argue his case with God, and wishes for an umpire between them. || Job longs for an “umpire” [[bible:Job 9:33]], a mediator who brings God and man together [[tosn:Job 9:32–33]]. Christians see this fulfilled in Christ [[tosn:Job 19:25]] [[bible:1 Timothy 2:5]].
10 | Job pours out his bitterness to God, who made him and now seems to hunt him. || Job pictures God as a potter shaping clay, yet still questions him [[tosn:Job 10:8–11]].
11 | Zophar rebukes Job sharply and says God has even forgotten part of his guilt. || Zophar, the harshest of the three friends, focuses only on sin [[tosn:Job 11:1]].
12 | Job answers that he too knows God’s power over all things, and mocks his friends’ wisdom. || “With God is wisdom and might” [[bible:Job 12:13]]. Job doubts the friends’ idea that wisdom simply comes with age [[tosn:Job 12:12]].
13 | Job says his friends speak falsely for God, and resolves to argue his case before God whatever it costs. || Job wants to “reason with God” himself [[bible:Job 13:3]]. The WEB reads 13:15 as “he will kill me. I have no hope. Nevertheless, I will maintain my ways before him” [[bible:Job 13:15]].
14 | “Man, who is born of a woman, is of few days, and full of trouble.” Job wonders, “If a man dies, will he live again?” || “If a man dies, will he live again?” [[bible:Job 14:14]]. The Old Testament recognised the LORD as Redeemer, and Christians know the Redeemer as Jesus Christ [[tosn:Job 19:25]].
15 | Eliphaz returns, accusing Job of undermining reverence for God. || In this second round the friends describe the fate of the wicked and suggest it is Job’s [[tosn:Job 15:1–21:34]].
16 | Job calls his friends “miserable comforters” but says, “My witness is in heaven.” || Job wishes for a heavenly advocate who would stand up for him [[tosn:Job 16:19–21]], a hope fulfilled only in Christ [[tosn:Job 19:25]].
17 | Job feels his spirit is broken and his hope buried. || Job no longer trusts his friends and thinks he will die without hope [[tosn:Job 17:10–16]].
18 | Bildad describes the terrible fate of the wicked. || “The lamp of the wicked” going out most likely means an early death [[tosn:Job 18:5]].
19 | Job feels abandoned by everyone, but declares, “I know that my Redeemer lives… then I will see God in my flesh.” || Job’s faith in a Redeemer could only be fulfilled in Christ [[tosn:Job 19:25]]. It is one of the most powerful statements in the book [[tbd:002:Book of Job]]. Job believed God would prove him right even if he died first [[tosn:Job 19:26]].
20 | Zophar describes how the joy of the wicked is short. || Zophar insists “the triumphing of the wicked is short” [[bible:Job 20:5]]. He speaks only twice, Eliphaz and Bildad three times each [[bsn:Job 4:1–31:40]].
21 | Job answers that in real life the wicked often prosper and die in peace. || Job disagrees with Bildad and the usual wisdom that the wicked’s lamp is soon put out [[tosn:Job 21:17]].
22 | Eliphaz accuses Job of specific sins against the poor and urges him to make peace with God. || In the third round the friends plainly accuse Job of being evil [[tosn:Job 22:1–26:14]].
23 | Job longs to find God and lay his case before him, but God seems absent. “He knows the way that I take.” || Job now feels confident he could present his case to God [[tosn:Job 23:2]]: “When he has tried me, I will come out like gold” [[bible:Job 23:10]].
24 | Job describes the misery of the poor and the apparent freedom of the wicked. || Job describes the desperate poor, including children seized for debt [[tosn:Job 24:9]] [[tosn:Job 24:6]].
25 | Bildad’s short final speech: how can a man be righteous before God? || Bildad argues no one can be fully pure before God [[tosn:Job 25:4]]. Zophar does not speak a third time [[bsn:Job 4:1–31:40]].
26 | Job describes God’s power over the deep and the heavens: “these are but the outskirts of his ways.” || “These are but the outskirts of his ways” [[bible:Job 26:14]].
27 | Job maintains his integrity and will not admit to sins he did not commit. || “Until I die I will not put away my integrity from me” [[bible:Job 27:5]].
28 | A poem on wisdom: people mine for gold and jewels, but where can wisdom be found? “The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom.” || No speaker is named, and some see it as the author’s own summary; it stresses the limits of human wisdom [[tosn:Job 28:1–28]]. God is the true source of wisdom [[tosn:Job 28:13]] [[bible:Job 28:28]].
29 | Job remembers his former life of honour, when he helped the poor and the orphan. || Job “was eyes to the blind, and feet to the lame” [[bible:Job 29:15]].
30 | Job laments that young men now mock him and God has thrown him into the mud. || Once the most important people praised him; now the least important mock him [[tosn:Job 30:2–3]].
31 | Job swears he is innocent of lust, deceit, injustice, neglecting the poor, and trusting in wealth, and challenges God to answer. || Job made a covenant with his eyes, guarding even his desires [[tosn:Job 31:1]] [[bible:Matthew 5:28]]. He also refused to treat his servants unjustly [[tosn:Job 31:13–15]].
32 | A young man, Elihu, angry with both Job and the friends, begins to speak. || After three rounds of speeches a young man, Elihu, speaks [[tosn:Job 32:1–37:24]]. He often says he is angry, probably seeing it as righteous zeal [[tosn:Job 32:2]].
33 | Elihu says God speaks through dreams and suffering to turn people from wrong. || “God speaks once, yes twice, though man pays no attention” [[bible:Job 33:14]]. Elihu thought Job was wrong to question God [[bsn:Job 32:1–37:24]].
34 | Elihu defends God’s justice and accuses Job of rebellion. || “Far be it from God, that he should do wickedness” [[bible:Job 34:10]].
35 | Elihu says Job’s righteousness cannot change God, and people cry out in pain without seeking God. || “Where is God my Maker, who gives songs in the night?” [[bible:Job 35:10]].
36 | Elihu says God uses affliction to open people’s ears, and describes God’s greatness in the storm. || “Who is a teacher like him?” [[bible:Job 36:22]]. Elihu claims his reasoning is flawless [[tosn:Job 36:4]].
37 | Elihu describes thunder, lightning and snow as the work of God, beyond our understanding. || “Stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God” [[bible:Job 37:14]].
38 | God answers Job from the whirlwind with questions: “Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?” || The Old Testament often links storms with God’s presence, and Job finally gets to speak with God [[tosn:Job 38:1–3]]. God gives no explanation for why the righteous suffer [[tbd:002:Book of Job]].
39 | God continues with wild animals: the mountain goat, the wild donkey, the ostrich, the horse and the eagle. || The wild animals scorn what is beneath them [[tosn:Job 39:5–7]]; the wild ox, never tamed, was a symbol of strength [[tosn:Job 39:9–12]].
40 | Job puts his hand over his mouth. God asks if Job would discredit God’s justice to justify himself, and describes Behemoth. || Behemoth and Leviathan are creatures humans cannot control [[tosn:Job 40:15–24]]. Here Behemoth appears to be a natural animal [[tosn:Job 40:15–24]].
41 | God describes Leviathan, a fearsome sea creature no human can tame. || Most scholars think Leviathan was like a crocodile [[tosn:Job 41:1]]. In the Bible this sea creature often stands for chaos and evil [[tosn:Job 41:1]] [[bible:Isaiah 27:1]].
42 | Job repents in dust and ashes: “I had heard of you… but now my eye sees you.” God rebukes the friends, for they did not speak rightly as Job did. Job prays for them, and God restores him. || Unlike his friends, Job spoke rightly about God [[tosn:Job 42:7]] [[bible:Job 42:7]]. James praises “the perseverance of Job” [[bible:James 5:11]].
