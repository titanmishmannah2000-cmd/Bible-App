# sourced
1 | Cyrus, king of Persia, decrees that the Jews may return to Jerusalem to rebuild the temple, and returns the temple vessels. || Cyrus conquered Babylon in October 539 BC [[tosn:Ezra 1:1]]. God moved this king, who did not know him, to issue the decree [[tosn:Ezra 1:1]], fulfilling Jeremiah’s word [[bible:Ezra 1:1]]. Isaiah had named Cyrus as God’s chosen instrument [[bible:Isaiah 44:28]].
2 | A list of the families who returned with Zerubbabel and Jeshua, about fifty thousand people. || The returning exiles needed to know who truly belonged to the community [[tosn:Ezra 2:1–70]]. A similar list, with some differences, is in Nehemiah 7 [[tosn:Nehemiah 7:8–38]].
3 | The altar is rebuilt and the temple foundation laid. The young shout for joy; the old who remembered the first temple weep. || “The people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping” [[bible:Ezra 3:13]].
4 | Enemies oppose the rebuilding and write to the Persian kings, and the work stops. || Aramaic was the diplomatic language of the Persian Empire, and Ezra copies the letters in Aramaic [[tosn:Ezra 4:7]] [[tosn:Ezra 4:11–22]]. Its Aramaic is very like fifth-century BC papyri from Elephantine in Egypt [[tbd:002:Book of Ezra]].
5 | The prophets Haggai and Zechariah urge the people to start building again. A governor writes to King Darius to check their permission. || Haggai first prophesied on August 29, 520 BC, and Zechariah about two months later; their books contain their messages [[tosn:Ezra 5:1]].
6 | Darius finds Cyrus’s decree, orders the work supported, and the temple is finished in 516 BC and dedicated with joy. The Passover is kept. || The second temple was finished about seventy years after Nebuchadnezzar destroyed the first in 586 BC [[tosn:Ezra 6:15]]. Herod the Great later remodelled it [[tosn:Ezra 6:15]].
7 | Ezra, a priest and a scribe expert in the law of Moses, comes to Jerusalem with the king’s support. || Ezra “set his heart to seek Yahweh’s law, and to do it, and to teach” [[bible:Ezra 7:10]]: study, obey, teach [[tosn:Ezra 7:10]]. From 7:27 to 9:15 Ezra tells his story in the first person [[tbd:002:Book of Ezra]].
8 | Ezra’s company travels safely after fasting and prayer, bringing gifts for the temple. || Ezra had told the king God would protect them, so asking for soldiers would have looked like doubting God [[tosn:Ezra 8:22]].
9 | Ezra learns that many have married women from surrounding peoples, tears his clothes, and confesses the sin of the people. || Israel was called to be a holy nation and not to take up pagan practices [[tosn:Ezra 9:1–2]]. Ezra confesses the sin as “our” guilt [[bible:Ezra 9:6]].
10 | The people agree to send away their foreign wives and children. || The people made a covenant to send away the pagan wives, renewing their commitment to the Sinai covenant [[tosn:Ezra 10:3]]. The law of Moses gave no clear instructions for this, so Ezra was asked to guide them [[tosn:Ezra 10:4]].
