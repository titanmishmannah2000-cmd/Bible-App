# sourced
1 | God creates the heavens and the earth in six days, bringing order and life out of darkness, and makes human beings, male and female, in his own image. Everything he makes is “very good”. || The sun and moon, chief gods of the nations around Israel, are not even named here, only called “lights” [[rsb:Gen.1.16]]. Humans are made to rule creation as God’s stewards, in language other ancient texts kept for kings [[rsb:Gen.1.26]] [[nabre:genesis/1]]. Christians read the chapter with John 1:1–3, where the Word was there in the beginning [[bible:John 1:1-3]].
2 | A closer look at the first man and woman: God forms Adam from the dust, plants a garden in Eden, gives one command, and makes Eve as his partner. God rests on the seventh day. || From 2:4 God is called “the LORD God” (Yahweh God). Many scholars take this as the start of an older tradition, called the Yahwist [[nabre:genesis/2]]. Jesus quotes 2:24 on marriage [[bible:Matthew 19:4-5]], and the river and tree of life return in the Bible’s last chapter [[bible:Revelation 22:1-2]] [[nabre:genesis/2]].
3 | The serpent tempts Eve and Adam to eat the forbidden fruit. Trust is broken, shame and blame follow, and they are sent out of the garden, but God clothes them and promises the serpent will be crushed. || Since Irenaeus in the 100s, Christians have read verse 15 as the first promise of a redeemer for fallen humankind, the “protoevangelium” (first gospel) [[nabre:genesis/3]]. Paul sets Adam’s disobedience beside Christ’s obedience [[bible:Romans 5:12-19]].
4 | Cain kills his brother Abel in anger and is sent away as a wanderer. Cain’s family builds the first city and invents music and metalwork, while violence grows; Seth is born to Adam and Eve. || Lamech’s boast shows Cain’s violence continuing and growing in his family [[nabre:genesis/4]] [[bible:Genesis 4:23-24]]. The family tree explains where the first crafts and culture came from [[nabre:genesis/4]]. Hebrews and 1 John look back on Abel and Cain [[bible:Hebrews 11:4]] [[bible:1 John 3:12]].
5 | A family tree from Adam to Noah, ten generations with very long lifespans, each ending “and he died”, except Enoch, who “walked with God” and was taken. || Ancient Mesopotamian king lists gave the kings before the flood reigns of 18,600 to 36,000 years; the ages in Genesis are much smaller [[nabre:genesis/5]]. Enoch, seventh in the ten-generation list, is understood to have been taken to God alive, like Elijah; he inspired many later writings [[nabre:genesis/5]] [[bible:Jude 1:14]].
6 | Human wickedness fills the earth and grieves God. He decides to send a flood, but Noah finds favour, and God tells him to build a great ship for his family and the animals. || The “sons of God” (6:2) have been read as the line of Seth (the traditional Christian view), as angels (the earliest Jewish view), or as tyrant kings [[rsb:Gen.6.2]]. The flood story draws on an ancient Mesopotamian flood tradition also found in the Epic of Gilgamesh and the Atrahasis Epic, but Genesis gives it a moral focus: human wickedness [[nabre:genesis/6]].
7 | Noah, his family and the animals enter the ship, rain falls for forty days, and the water covers the earth, destroying all life outside. || The “fountains of the great deep” are the ocean under the earth from 1:2; with the windows of the sky they burst open (7:11) [[nabre:genesis/7]] [[bible:Genesis 7:11]]. Peter calls the flood a picture of baptism [[bible:1 Peter 3:20-21]].
8 | The waters go down, Noah sends out a raven and a dove, and the family leaves the ship. Noah offers a sacrifice and God promises never again to destroy the earth by flood. || When the water dries, the world is back where it stood on the day of creation, with dry land appearing as in 1:9 [[nabre:genesis/8]]. God promises never again to curse the ground, even though “the imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth” [[bible:Genesis 8:21]].
9 | God blesses Noah and makes a covenant with every living creature, with the rainbow as its sign. Afterwards Noah gets drunk and his son Ham shames him. || God makes his covenant not only with Noah and his family but, remarkably, with every animal that came out of the ark; the rainbow is its sign [[nabre:genesis/9]].
10 | The Table of Nations: the descendants of Noah’s sons Shem, Ham and Japheth spread out to become the peoples of the ancient world. || The nations listed come to seventy, a traditional number in the Bible for completeness [[nabre:genesis/10]]. Later, Israel’s seventy people who go down to Egypt stand for the nations before God [[nabre:genesis/46]].
11 | People build a tower at Babel to make a name for themselves, and God scatters them by confusing their language. Then a family tree leads to Abram. || Babel is the Hebrew form of the name Babylon, and the tower is possibly Babylon’s great temple-tower (ziggurat), E-sag-ila [[nabre:genesis/11]].
12 | God calls Abram to leave his country for a land he will show him, promising to make him a great nation and to bless all the families of the earth through him. In Egypt Abram lies about his wife Sarai. || God’s call of Abram begins a new history of blessing that reverses Babel: Abram leaves home, and God, not people, makes his name great [[nabre:genesis/12]]. Paul says the Good News was preached to Abraham beforehand in 12:3 [[bible:Galatians 3:8]]. The story of Sarah in danger in Egypt is told again in chapter 20 and with Rebekah in 26 [[nabre:genesis/12]].
13 | Abram and his nephew Lot separate; Lot chooses the rich plain near Sodom, and God renews his promise of land and countless descendants to Abram. || Abraham settles the quarrel with trust in God and generosity to his nephew: Lot chooses for himself, while Abraham waits for God to show him the land [[nabre:genesis/13]].
14 | Abram rescues Lot from invading kings, then is blessed by Melchizedek, the mysterious priest-king of Salem, and gives him a tenth. || Salem is Jerusalem [[nabre:genesis/14]]. Melchizedek appears again in Psalm 110:4 [[bible:Psalm 110:4]], and Hebrews 7 presents him as a picture (prefiguration) of Christ [[nabre:genesis/14]].
15 | God promises Abram a son and descendants as many as the stars. Abram believes, and it is counted to him as righteousness. God seals a covenant in a strange night ceremony. || Paul makes Abraham’s faith in 15:6 a model for Christians [[nabre:genesis/15]] [[bible:Romans 4:3]] [[bible:Galatians 3:6]], and James quotes it too [[bible:James 2:23]]. Cutting animals in two was an ancient way to seal a treaty, invoking the animals’ fate on anyone who broke it. Here only the fire pot and torch, representing God, pass between the pieces [[nabre:genesis/15]].
16 | Sarai gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to Abram, and Hagar conceives. Mistreated, Hagar flees, and the angel of Yahweh finds her; she names God “You are a God who sees”. Ishmael is born. || Giving an infertile wife’s servant to her husband was a common custom in ancient Near Eastern law [[nabre:genesis/16]]. “The angel of Yahweh” is God appearing in human form [[nabre:genesis/16]]. Ishmael means “God has heard”, and Hagar’s name for God, “You are a God who sees”, appears nowhere else [[nabre:genesis/16]] [[rsb:Gen.16.13]]. Paul uses Hagar and Sarah as an allegory of two covenants [[bible:Galatians 4:22-24]].
17 | God renames Abram Abraham and Sarai Sarah, promises a son through Sarah, and gives circumcision as the sign of the covenant. || Abram and Abraham are two forms of one name meaning “the father is exalted”; the longer form was popularly heard as “father of a multitude of nations” [[nabre:genesis/17]]. Circumcision was widely practised in the ancient world, but moving it to the eighth day made it the sign of God’s covenant [[nabre:genesis/17]]. Paul notes Abraham received this sign after he was counted righteous [[bible:Romans 4:9-11]].
18 | Three visitors come to Abraham; one is Yahweh, who promises Sarah a son within a year, and she laughs. Abraham pleads with God to spare Sodom for the sake of the righteous. || The shifting between three visitors and the LORD is the story’s way of expressing God’s mysterious presence [[nabre:genesis/18]]. Abraham questions God about the justice of destroying Sodom [[nabre:genesis/18]]. Hebrews recalls those who “entertained angels without knowing it” [[bible:Hebrews 13:2]].
19 | Angels rescue Lot from Sodom before God destroys the city with fire. Lot’s wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt; Lot’s daughters later father children by him. || Genesis presents Sodom’s sin as a violent violation of the sacred duty of hospitality [[nabre:genesis/18]] [[nabre:genesis/19]]; Ezekiel names its pride, excess and neglect of the poor [[bible:Ezekiel 16:49]]. Jesus says, “Remember Lot’s wife!” [[bible:Luke 17:32]]. The ending tells the origin of Moab and Ammon, Israel’s rival neighbours [[nabre:genesis/19]].
20 | Abraham again passes off Sarah as his sister, this time with King Abimelech of Gerar, and God protects her. || Abraham again passes Sarah off as his sister, as in chapter 12; Isaac does the same in 26 [[nabre:genesis/20]]. Only here is Abraham explicitly called a prophet [[nabre:genesis/20]].
21 | Isaac is born, and Sarah laughs for joy. Hagar and Ishmael are sent away, but God provides water in the desert and promises Ishmael a nation. || The name Isaac plays on the Hebrew words for laughter, the third laugh in the story after 17:17 and 18:12 [[nabre:genesis/21]]. God promises to make a nation of Ishmael too [[nabre:genesis/21]] [[bible:Genesis 25:12-18]].
22 | God tests Abraham by telling him to offer Isaac as a sacrifice. At the last moment God stops him and provides a ram instead. Abraham names the place “Yahweh will provide”. || Jewish tradition calls this the Akedah, “the binding” of Isaac [[nabre:genesis/22]]. Hebrews says Abraham reasoned that God could raise the dead [[bible:Hebrews 11:17-19]]. 2 Chronicles places Mount Moriah where Solomon built the temple [[nabre:genesis/22]] [[bible:2 Chronicles 3:1]].
23 | Sarah dies, and Abraham buys a cave at Machpelah near Hebron to bury her, his first piece of the promised land. || A resident foreigner normally had no right to own land, so this purchase, worded in legal terms, gives Abraham’s family its first legal hold in the promised land [[nabre:genesis/23]]. The 400 shekels were probably an exorbitant price [[nabre:genesis/23]].
24 | Abraham’s servant travels to find a wife for Isaac among his relatives, prays for a sign, and meets Rebekah at a well. She agrees to go, and Isaac loves her. || The story is told at extraordinary length, with the sign the servant asks for and its fulfilment described in detail [[nabre:genesis/24]]. Isaac’s wife must come from his own people so that the promises can be fulfilled [[nabre:genesis/24]].
25 | Abraham dies. Rebekah has twins, Esau and Jacob, who struggle even in the womb, and Esau sells his birthright to Jacob for a bowl of stew. || Only Rebekah is told that the elder will serve the younger [[nabre:genesis/25]]; Paul quotes the line in Romans 9 [[bible:Romans 9:10-13]]. “Red” plays on Edom, Esau’s other name, and “Jacob” on the word for heel [[nabre:genesis/25]]. The birthright meant honour and a double share of the inheritance [[nabre:genesis/25]].
26 | God renews the promise to Isaac. Isaac repeats his father’s lie about his wife, then quarrels over wells with the Philistines before making peace. || The promises to Abraham are renewed for Isaac [[nabre:genesis/26]]. This is the third telling of the wife-in-danger story [[nabre:genesis/26]]. In a dry land, rights to wells were a kind of claim on the land itself [[nabre:genesis/26]].
27 | Rebekah and Jacob trick blind Isaac into blessing Jacob instead of Esau. Esau vows to kill his brother, and Jacob must flee. || A literary masterpiece, managed by Rebekah: Jacob takes Esau’s blessing, which gives fertility and rule [[nabre:genesis/27]]. In Hebrew “birthright” (bekorah) and “blessing” (berakah) sound alike [[nabre:genesis/27]]. Hebrews warns against Esau’s careless loss [[bible:Hebrews 12:16-17]].
28 | Running away, Jacob dreams of a stairway between heaven and earth with angels on it, and God promises to be with him. He names the place Bethel, “house of God”. || The “ladder” is better a stairway or ramp, an image that may come from the Babylonian temple-towers [[nabre:genesis/28]]. Bethel means “house of God” [[nabre:genesis/28]]. Jesus echoes the dream: angels ascending and descending on the Son of Man [[bible:John 1:51]].
29 | Jacob meets Rachel at a well and works seven years for her, but her father Laban tricks him into marrying her sister Leah first. Leah bears four sons, including Levi and Judah. || The great irony: Jacob, who deceived his father and brother, is deceived by his uncle [[nabre:genesis/29]]. Jacob’s neglect of Leah moves God to give her children, and each son’s name puns on her feelings: Reuben, “he saw my misery”, Judah, “praise” [[nabre:genesis/29]].
30 | A rivalry between Leah and Rachel as both, with their servants, bear Jacob children; Rachel finally has Joseph. Jacob grows rich with flocks. || The sons’ names are puns on the mothers’ words [[nabre:genesis/30]]. Mandrakes were thought to help conception [[nabre:genesis/30]]. Jacob’s trick with peeled branches reflects a folk belief, but the story makes clear God is behind the increase [[nabre:genesis/30]] [[bible:Genesis 31:9]].
31 | Jacob secretly leaves Laban with his family and flocks; Rachel steals her father’s household idols. Laban catches up, and they make a covenant of peace at a heap of stones. || The household gods (teraphim) were figurines used in divination [[nabre:genesis/31]]. The Mizpah heap seals a typical treaty between two parties, setting a boundary neither will cross to harm the other [[nabre:genesis/31]].
32 | Jacob fears meeting Esau. Alone at night by the river Jabbok, he wrestles with a mysterious man until dawn, is given the name Israel, and leaves limping. || The name Israel is explained from Hebrew roots meaning “you contended” and “divine beings” [[nabre:genesis/32]]. The wrestler’s identity is left fluid, suggesting God’s presence; Hosea calls him an angel [[nabre:genesis/32]] [[bible:Hosea 12:4]]. Peniel means “face of God” [[nabre:genesis/32]].
33 | Esau runs to meet Jacob and embraces him, and the brothers are reconciled. Jacob settles near Shechem. || The meeting brings reconciliation: Esau proves large-hearted, and Jacob’s gift is called a “blessing”, though the parting shows the peace is not complete [[nabre:genesis/33]].
34 | Jacob’s daughter Dinah is raped by Shechem, and her brothers Simeon and Levi take a brutal revenge on the whole city. || The story may reflect how the tribes of Simeon and Levi related to their Canaanite neighbours [[nabre:genesis/34]]. Jacob later condemns Simeon and Levi’s anger [[bible:Genesis 49:5-7]] [[nabre:genesis/49]].
35 | Jacob returns to Bethel, buries the family’s idols, and God renews the promise. Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin, and Isaac dies. || Before worship at Bethel the family gets rid of their foreign gods [[nabre:genesis/35]]. “Ben-oni” means “son of my affliction” [[nabre:genesis/35]]. The words “Ephrath (that is, Bethlehem)” are thought to be a later note; other passages place Rachel’s grave near Ramah, north of Jerusalem [[nabre:genesis/35]] [[bible:Jeremiah 31:15]], a verse Matthew recalls [[bible:Matthew 2:18]].
36 | The family tree of Esau, the ancestor of the Edomites, and the kings who ruled Edom. || 36:31 speaks of kings in Edom “before any king reigned over the children of Israel”, so it was written after Saul became Israel’s first king [[nabre:genesis/36]].
37 | Joseph, Jacob’s favourite son, dreams of ruling over his brothers. Jealous, they sell him to traders bound for Egypt and tell their father he was killed by an animal. || The Joseph story (37–50) explains how Israel came to Egypt, with a divine purpose moving events to their end [[nabre:genesis/37]]. The special coat is “of many colours” in the ancient Greek translation; the Hebrew may mean “a long robe with sleeves”, and it marked Joseph as his father’s favourite [[rsb:Gen.37.3]] [[nabre:genesis/37]]. In one strand of the story Reuben tries to rescue Joseph, in the other Judah [[nabre:genesis/37]].
38 | Judah wrongs his widowed daughter-in-law Tamar, who disguises herself to secure her right to a child; Judah admits, “She is more righteous than I.” || The chapter has subtle links to the Joseph story, such as deception by means of clothing [[nabre:genesis/38]]. Judah’s duty to “raise up offspring” for his dead son refers to levirate marriage [[nabre:genesis/38]]. Perez means “breach” [[nabre:genesis/38]], and he is an ancestor of Jesus in Matthew’s family tree [[bible:Matthew 1:3]].
39 | In Egypt Joseph serves Potiphar faithfully, refuses the advances of Potiphar’s wife, and is thrown into prison on a false charge, yet “Yahweh was with Joseph”. || The chapter repeats that “Yahweh was with Joseph”, the same phrase used of Samuel and David [[nabre:genesis/39]] [[bible:Genesis 39:2]].
40 | In prison Joseph interprets the dreams of Pharaoh’s cupbearer and baker. Both come true, but the cupbearer forgets him. || Joseph’s gift of interpreting dreams shows that God is still with him, and it points ahead to chapter 41 [[nabre:genesis/40]]. He insists, “Don’t interpretations belong to God?” [[bible:Genesis 40:8]]
41 | Joseph interprets Pharaoh’s dreams of seven good years and seven years of famine, and Pharaoh makes him ruler over Egypt to prepare. || Egypt had a long tradition of dream interpretation, with manuals listing dreams as good or bad [[nabre:genesis/41]]. Pharaoh’s signet ring made Joseph vizier, and his Egyptian name and his wife’s family names are genuinely Egyptian [[nabre:genesis/41]].
42 | Famine drives Joseph’s brothers to Egypt to buy grain. Joseph recognizes them, though they do not know him, and tests them by keeping Simeon. || Famine brings the brothers to Egypt, as it once brought Abraham and Sarah, and Joseph begins a process of discipline for his brothers [[nabre:genesis/42]]. They begin to admit their guilt over what they did to him [[bible:Genesis 42:21]].
43 | The brothers return with Benjamin, Rachel’s other son. Joseph feasts with them and has to leave the room to weep. || Judah now steps forward as the hero, offering himself as guarantee for Benjamin; in chapters 37 and 42 that role was Reuben’s [[nabre:genesis/43]].
44 | Joseph frames Benjamin with a silver cup, and Judah offers to become a slave in his place to spare his father grief. || Judah’s plea is the longest speech in Genesis, and he now takes Reuben’s place as the brothers’ spokesman [[nabre:genesis/44]]. The brother who once helped sell Joseph offers to stay as a slave in Benjamin’s place [[bible:Genesis 37:26-27]] [[bible:Genesis 44:33]].
45 | Joseph reveals himself to his brothers: “it wasn’t you who sent me here, but God.” He invites the whole family to Egypt. || “Father to Pharaoh” was a title for the vizier [[nabre:genesis/45]]. Joseph says God sent him ahead to save lives, a theme repeated in 50:20 [[bible:Genesis 45:5-8]] [[nabre:genesis/45]].
46 | God tells Jacob not to fear going down to Egypt. Seventy members of the family travel there, and Jacob is reunited with Joseph. || The count of seventy is best read as expressing totality: Israel in miniature, standing for the seventy nations of Genesis 10 [[nabre:genesis/46]]. Exodus begins where this list ends [[bible:Exodus 1:1-5]].
47 | Jacob blesses Pharaoh, the family settles in Goshen, and Joseph’s famine policy brings the Egyptians into servitude to Pharaoh. || Joseph’s policy leaves the people in servitude, yet they see him as their saviour, not a tyrant [[rsb:Gen.47.21]] [[bible:Genesis 47:25]]. Jacob calls life a pilgrimage: people are only sojourners on the earth [[nabre:genesis/47]].
48 | Jacob adopts Joseph’s sons Ephraim and Manasseh and, crossing his hands, blesses the younger above the elder. || Jacob adopts Joseph’s two sons, raising them to the same rank as his own firstborn sons, Reuben and Simeon [[nabre:genesis/48]] [[bible:Genesis 48:5]]. As Isaac once did, he blesses the younger before the elder [[nabre:genesis/48]] [[bible:Genesis 48:14]].
49 | On his deathbed Jacob speaks a poem over each of his twelve sons, foretelling their tribes’ futures; Judah is promised the scepter. || The poetry is dense and archaic [[nabre:genesis/49]]. 49:10, “until he comes to whom it belongs”, has traditionally been read as messianic; its main point is the rule of Judah and David’s line [[nabre:genesis/49]].
50 | Jacob is buried in Canaan. Joseph reassures his brothers: “You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good.” Joseph dies in Egypt, asking that his bones be carried to the promised land. || Joseph’s words, “you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good”, sum up the whole story [[nabre:genesis/50]]. His bones are later carried out of Egypt and buried at Shechem [[bible:Exodus 13:19]] [[bible:Joshua 24:32]].
