1 | God sends Jonah to warn Nineveh, the capital of the brutal Assyrian empire, but Jonah runs the other way by ship. In a storm the pagan sailors pray while Jonah sleeps; he is thrown overboard, and a great fish swallows him. || Tarshish may have been Tartessos in Spain, as far as possible in the opposite direction from Nineveh [[tosn:Jonah 1:3]]. Jonah’s God made the sea and the land, unlike the sailors’ gods [[tosn:Jonah 1:9]]. Jesus made Jonah’s three days in the fish a sign of his own death and resurrection [[tbd:002:Book of Jonah]] [[bible:Matthew 12:40]].
2 | From inside the fish Jonah prays a psalm of thanks: “Salvation belongs to Yahweh.” The fish spits him out on dry land. || Jonah’s prayer follows the pattern of a personal song of thanks, like a psalm [[tosn:Jonah 2:1–9]]; it recalls the cry he made as he was drowning [[tosn:Jonah 2:2]].
3 | Jonah preaches, “In forty days, Nineveh will be overthrown!” The whole city, from king to cattle, repents in sackcloth, and God relents. || Ironically the city repents, just as Jonah feared [[tosn:Jonah 3:1–4.11]]. Fasting and sackcloth showed deep repentance [[tosn:Jonah 3:5–6]]. Jesus said Nineveh’s repentance exposed the unbelief of his own generation [[tbd:002:Book of Jonah]] [[bible:Luke 11:32]].
4 | Jonah is furious that God spared Nineveh: “I knew that you are a gracious God.” God grows a plant to shade him, then lets it wither, and asks, “Shouldn’t I be concerned for Nineveh, that great city?” || Jonah almost quotes Exodus 34:6–7, God’s description of himself to Moses [[tosn:Jonah 4:2]] [[bsn:Jonah 4:1–11]]. God’s closing question rebukes Jonah’s exclusive attitude, one of the book’s key lessons [[tbd:002:Book of Jonah]].
