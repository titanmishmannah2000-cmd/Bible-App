1 | Paul greets the Roman church and says he is not ashamed of the good news, “the power of God for salvation for everyone who believes.” Humanity has exchanged the truth of God for idols. || 1:16–17 states the theme of the letter, quoting Habakkuk 2:4.
2 | God judges everyone impartially, Jews and Gentiles alike. True circumcision is of the heart. || Paul turns to the moral critic: those who judge others do the same things.
3 | “All have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God.” But now God’s righteousness is revealed through faith in Jesus Christ, and people are justified freely by his grace. || 3:21–26 is often called the heart of Romans. Paul quotes a chain of psalms to show universal sin.
4 | Abraham was counted righteous by faith before he was circumcised, so he is the father of all who believe. || Paul quotes Genesis 15:6 and Psalm 32. Abraham believed God who “gives life to the dead”.
5 | Being justified by faith, we have peace with God. “God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.” Adam brought death; Christ brings life. || The Adam–Christ contrast shaped the doctrine of original sin (5:12–21).
6 | Shall we keep sinning so grace may increase? No: in baptism we died with Christ and rose to new life. “The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life.” || Union with Christ is the root of Christian living.
7 | Believers are freed from the law. Paul describes the struggle: “The good which I desire, I don’t do.” “Who will deliver me?… I thank God through Jesus Christ.” || Whether Paul describes his life before or after conversion has been debated since Augustine.
8 | “There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.” The Spirit makes us children of God who cry “Abba, Father”. Creation groans for redemption. “All things work together for good.” Nothing can separate us from God’s love. || Often called the summit of the letter. The Spirit is mentioned about twenty times.
9 | Paul grieves for his fellow Jews. God’s promise has not failed: not all descended from Israel are Israel. God has mercy on whom he will have mercy. || A hard chapter on election, central to debates between Calvinists and Arminians.
10 | “If you will confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.” “How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the Good News of peace” || The earliest Christian confession: “Jesus is Lord”. Paul quotes Deuteronomy 30 and Isaiah 52.
11 | God has not rejected Israel. Gentiles are grafted into the olive tree; one day “all Israel will be saved”. “Oh the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and the knowledge of God!” || The olive tree warns Gentile believers against pride. The meaning of “all Israel” is debated.
12 | “Present your bodies a living sacrifice.” Be transformed by renewing your mind. Use your gifts. Love sincerely; bless those who persecute you; overcome evil with good. || The turn from teaching to living; the “therefore” of 12:1 links them.
13 | Submit to governing authorities. “Owe no one anything, except to love one another.” Put on the Lord Jesus Christ. || 13:1–7 has been central to Christian thinking about the state. Augustine was converted reading 13:13–14.
14 | Don’t judge one another over food or special days. Each will give account to God; don’t make others stumble. || A guide for unity amid differences of conscience.
15 | The strong should bear with the weak, as Christ did. Paul’s mission plans: to Jerusalem with a gift, then to Rome and Spain. || Paul quotes four Old Testament texts about Gentiles praising God.
16 | Greetings to many friends, including Phoebe, a deacon, Prisca and Aquila, and Junia, “notable among the apostles”. || Many women are named as co-workers. The list gives a glimpse of a diverse early church.
