# sourced
1 | Hannah, childless and taunted, prays at Shiloh, and the priest Eli thinks she is drunk. God answers, she bears Samuel, and she gives him to serve God. || Hannah could not have children, like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and the mother of Samson before her [[bsn:1 Samuel 1:1–2:11]]. The name Samuel probably means “name of God” or “his name is El”; Hannah’s words in 1:20 are a play on the sound of the name rather than its meaning [[tbd:019:Samuel (Person)]].
2 | Hannah’s song praises God who lifts the poor from the dust. Eli’s sons abuse the priesthood, while young Samuel grows in favour. || Like Mary’s song in Luke 1:46–55, Hannah’s prayer celebrates God bringing down the powerful and lifting up the poor [[tosn:1 Samuel 2:1–10]]. Her words about God’s “anointed” look ahead to Israel’s kings and ultimately to Christ [[tosn:1 Samuel 2:10]]. Samuel growing in favour is echoed of the boy Jesus [[tosn:1 Samuel 2:26]] [[bible:Luke 2:52]].
3 | God calls the boy Samuel by name in the night; Samuel answers, “Speak; for your servant hears.” He becomes known as a prophet. || “Yahweh’s word was rare in those days” [[bible:1 Samuel 3:1]], meaning God was not regularly speaking to his people [[tosn:1 Samuel 3:1]]. Now none of Samuel’s words “fall to the ground”: he is a true prophet [[tosn:1 Samuel 3:19]].
4 | The Philistines defeat Israel, capture the ark, and Eli’s sons are killed. Eli falls and dies, and a baby is named Ichabod, “the glory has departed”. || Both Israel and the Philistines treated the ark like a powerful charm [[tosn:1 Samuel 4:6–7]]. The far greater defeat taught Israel that the ark was not a magical object [[tosn:1 Samuel 4:10]].
5 | The ark brings disaster on the Philistines: their god Dagon falls before it and plagues strike their cities. || Dagon was a Philistine god [[tosn:1 Samuel 5:2]] [[bible:Judges 16:23]]. The Philistines’ real mistake was worshipping an idol instead of the one true God [[tosn:1 Samuel 5:5]].
6 | The Philistines send the ark back on a cart pulled by two cows, with gold offerings; it comes to Beth Shemesh. || If the cows left their own calves to take the ark to Israel, it would show that the plague came from Israel’s God [[tosn:1 Samuel 6:7]] [[bible:1 Samuel 6:9]].
7 | Samuel calls Israel to put away foreign gods; God thunders against the Philistines. Samuel sets up a stone called Ebenezer. || Samuel named the stone with the words “Yahweh helped us until now” [[bible:1 Samuel 7:12]].
8 | Israel asks Samuel for a king “like all the nations”. God says they have rejected him, and Samuel warns what a king will take. || In asking for a king they were rejecting God’s own rule [[tosn:1 Samuel 8:7]]. The book shows kingship as the people demanded it denying the covenant, yet kingship as Samuel set it up fitting the covenant [[tbd:019:Samuel, Books of First and Second]].
9 | Saul, a tall young man looking for lost donkeys, meets Samuel, who has been told by God to anoint him. || God guided Saul to Samuel’s home town [[tosn:1 Samuel 9:5]]. Anointing with olive oil marked someone set apart for a special purpose [[tosn:1 Samuel 9:16]].
10 | Samuel anoints Saul, the Spirit comes on him and he prophesies. Saul is chosen by lot but hides among the baggage. || “Is Saul also among the prophets?” became a proverb [[bible:1 Samuel 10:11-12]].
11 | Saul rescues the town of Jabesh Gilead from the Ammonites, and the kingship is renewed. || Saul began as a humble king, and all Israel accepted him after this rescue [[bsn:1 Samuel 8:1–12:25]]. The men of Jabesh Gilead later repay him (31:11–13) [[bible:1 Samuel 31:11-13]].
12 | Samuel gives a farewell speech, defends his integrity, and urges king and people to follow God. || The book’s mixed feelings about kingship are resolved in this chapter [[tbd:019:Samuel, Books of First and Second]]. Samuel promises to keep praying for them [[bible:1 Samuel 12:23]].
13 | Saul offers a sacrifice instead of waiting for Samuel, and Samuel says God will seek “a man after his own heart”. || The text giving Saul’s age and length of reign in 13:1 has a copying problem [[tosn:1 Samuel 13:1]]. The “man after his own heart” is David [[tosn:1 Samuel 13:14]] [[bible:Acts 13:22]].
14 | Jonathan and his armour-bearer attack a Philistine outpost; Saul’s rash oath nearly gets Jonathan killed. || Jonathan trusts that “there is no restraint on Yahweh to save by many or by few” [[bible:1 Samuel 14:6]]. Saul’s actions here further support his removal as king, and he is cut off from both Samuel and his brave son [[tosn:1 Samuel 14:1–52]].
15 | Saul disobeys God’s command against the Amalekites and keeps the best spoil. Samuel says, “To obey is better than sacrifice.” God rejects Saul as king. || Obedience over ritual is a theme taken up again in Psalm 40:6, Hosea 6:6 and by Jesus in Matthew 12:7 [[tosn:1 Samuel 15:22]].
16 | Samuel anoints the young shepherd David, for “Yahweh looks at the heart”. David plays the harp to calm Saul. || Both Saul and David were unlikely choices [[tosn:1 Samuel 16:1–23]]. The Spirit comes on David and leaves Saul [[bible:1 Samuel 16:13-14]].
17 | David, armed with a sling, defeats the Philistine giant Goliath, saying, “The battle is Yahweh’s.” || Settling a war by a fight between two champions was a known custom [[tosn:1 Samuel 17:4]]. 2 Samuel 21:19 says Elhanan killed Goliath, while 1 Chronicles 20:5 says Elhanan killed Goliath’s brother [[tbd:007:Goliath]].
18 | Jonathan loves David as himself. Saul grows jealous of David’s success and tries to kill him. || The women sing, “Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands” [[bible:1 Samuel 18:7]], and Saul grows jealous [[bible:1 Samuel 18:8-9]]. Jonathan and David were alike in many ways, and each had a claim to the throne [[tosn:1 Samuel 18:1]].
19 | Saul tries again to kill David, but Jonathan and David’s wife Michal help him escape to Samuel. || Each time Saul sends men, God’s Spirit overcomes them and they prophesy instead of capturing David [[tosn:1 Samuel 19:21]]. Saul lying stripped of his royal clothes shows that God had stripped him of the kingship [[tosn:1 Samuel 19:24]].
20 | Jonathan confirms that Saul means to kill David, and the friends part with a covenant and tears. || Jonathan and David bind themselves in a covenant before God [[tosn:1 Samuel 20:8]]. Later Jonathan tells David, “you will be king over Israel, and I will be next to you” [[bible:1 Samuel 23:17]].
21 | David flees, eats the holy bread at Nob, and pretends to be mad before the Philistine king of Gath. || Jesus cites David eating the holy bread [[bible:Mark 2:25-26]]. The title of Psalm 34 links it to this episode [[tosn:1 Samuel 21:12–13]] [[bible:Psalm 34]].
22 | David gathers four hundred outcasts at the cave of Adullam. Saul has the priests of Nob massacred. || By drawing the distressed, the indebted and the discontented to himself, David pointed ahead to Jesus, who welcomed the hurting and burdened [[tosn:1 Samuel 22:2]].
23 | David rescues the town of Keilah, and Saul hunts him in the wilderness; Jonathan strengthens David’s hand in God. || David asks the LORD what to do before he acts [[tosn:1 Samuel 23:4]] [[bible:1 Samuel 23:2-4]].
24 | David spares Saul’s life in a cave at En Gedi, cutting only a corner of his robe. || The LORD’s anointed was not to be harmed, even though God had chosen David as the next king [[tosn:1 Samuel 24:6]]. The title of Psalm 57 places it in the cave [[bible:Psalm 57]].
25 | Samuel dies. Abigail’s wisdom stops David from taking revenge on her foolish husband Nabal, and David later marries her. || Abigail herself says of Nabal, “as his name is, so is he” [[bible:1 Samuel 25:25]] [[tosn:1 Samuel 25:3]].
26 | David spares Saul again, taking his spear and water jug while he sleeps. || Saul admits, “I have played the fool” [[bible:1 Samuel 26:21]], and David again refuses to harm the LORD’s anointed [[tosn:1 Samuel 24:6]].
27 | David takes refuge among the Philistines in Ziklag, deceiving King Achish. || This was David’s second stay with Achish, and both times he lied to him [[tosn:1 Samuel 27:2]]. Having to live outside Israel was one of the covenant curses [[bsn:1 Samuel 27:1–31:13]].
28 | Desperate before battle, Saul consults a medium at Endor, who brings up Samuel, who foretells Saul’s death. || Saul had no answer from God by dreams, prophets or lots, because he had refused what God already told him through Samuel [[bsn:1 Samuel 27:1–31:13]]. The medium describes “a god” coming up; what exactly she saw is not explained [[tosn:1 Samuel 28:13]].
29 | The Philistine commanders do not trust David and send him back from the battle. || God’s help saved David from having to fight his own people [[tosn:1 Samuel 29:1–11]].
30 | Amalekites raid Ziklag. “David strengthened himself in Yahweh his God,” pursues them and recovers everything. || David rules that those who stayed with the baggage “shall share alike” [[bible:1 Samuel 30:24]], like Moses’ division of plunder in Numbers 31 [[tosn:1 Samuel 30:24]].
31 | The Philistines defeat Israel on Mount Gilboa; Saul and Jonathan die. The men of Jabesh Gilead rescue their bodies. || Saul’s reign failed to match what God required of a covenant king [[tbd:019:Samuel, Books of First and Second]]. The men of Jabesh repay Saul’s rescue in chapter 11 [[bible:1 Samuel 31:11-13]].
