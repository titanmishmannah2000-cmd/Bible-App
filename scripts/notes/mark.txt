1 | John the Baptizer prepares the way, Jesus is baptized and tested in the wilderness, then calls fishermen to follow him and heals many people in Galilee. || Mark has no birth story; it opens with Isaiah and the voice in the wilderness. “Immediately” appears again and again, giving the Gospel its urgent pace. Jesus’s message is summed up in 1:15: the Kingdom of God is near.
2 | Jesus forgives and heals a paralysed man let down through a roof, calls the tax collector Levi, eats with sinners, and claims authority over the Sabbath. || The first of five conflict stories (2:1–3:6). By forgiving sins Jesus claims what only God can do, and he calls himself “the Son of Man”.
3 | Jesus heals on the Sabbath, angering the Pharisees, chooses twelve apostles, and answers the charge that he drives out demons by Satan’s power. His family comes to take him home. || Mark “sandwiches” stories: the family scene wraps around the Beelzebul controversy. Jesus redefines family as those who do God’s will.
4 | Jesus teaches in parables: the sower, the lamp, the growing seed and the mustard seed. Then he calms a storm on the lake, and the disciples ask, “Who is this?” || Parables both reveal and conceal (4:11–12, quoting Isaiah 6). Calming the sea recalls God’s power over the waters in the Psalms.
5 | Jesus frees a man possessed by a “Legion” of demons, heals a woman who touches his cloak, and raises the daughter of Jairus, a synagogue leader. || Another sandwich story: the woman, unclean for twelve years, and the girl, twelve years old, are both made whole. Jesus’s Aramaic words “Talitha cumi” are kept.
6 | Jesus is rejected in Nazareth, sends out the Twelve, and feeds five thousand people with five loaves and two fish. Herod has John the Baptizer beheaded, and Jesus walks on the water. || Jesus is called “the carpenter, the son of Mary” (6:3). The feeding in the wilderness recalls the manna and Psalm 23, with “green grass” and sheep without a shepherd.
7 | Jesus challenges traditions about washing, teaching that evil comes from within, then heals the daughter of a Greek woman and a deaf man. || Mark explains Jewish customs for non-Jewish readers. Mark adds “making all foods clean” (7:19). The Syrophoenician woman’s answer is one of the few times someone wins an argument with Jesus.
8 | Jesus feeds four thousand, heals a blind man in two stages, and Peter declares, “You are the Christ.” Jesus then foretells his death and resurrection and calls followers to take up their cross. || The hinge of Mark. The two-stage healing pictures the disciples, who see Jesus is the Christ but not yet what that means.
9 | On a high mountain Jesus is transfigured before Peter, James and John. He heals a boy whose father cries, “I believe; help my unbelief,” and teaches that the greatest must be the servant of all. || Moses and Elijah represent the Law and the Prophets. The heavenly voice echoes the baptism and adds, “Listen to him”, recalling Deuteronomy 18:15.
10 | Jesus teaches about divorce, blesses children, and tells a rich man to sell everything and follow him. He says he came “to give his life as a ransom for many”, and heals blind Bartimaeus. || 10:45 is a key statement of why Jesus came, drawing on Isaiah 53. Bartimaeus, who “followed Jesus on the way”, is a model disciple.
11 | Jesus rides into Jerusalem on a colt to shouts of “Hosanna!”, curses a fig tree, and drives traders from the temple, calling it a house of prayer. || The entry acts out Zechariah 9:9. The withered fig tree wraps around the temple scene, a sign of judgement on fruitless worship.
12 | Jesus tells the parable of the wicked tenants and answers trick questions about taxes, the resurrection and the greatest commandment. He praises a poor widow’s gift. || The greatest commandment joins Deuteronomy 6:4–5 (the Shema) and Leviticus 19:18. “Render to Caesar” has shaped Christian thinking on church and state.
13 | Jesus foretells the destruction of the temple and speaks of wars, persecution and the coming of the Son of Man, urging his followers to watch. || Called the “Little Apocalypse”. Scholars debate how much refers to the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70 and how much to the end of the age.
14 | A woman anoints Jesus, Judas agrees to betray him, and Jesus shares the Passover meal, giving bread and wine as his body and blood. He prays in Gethsemane, is arrested, tried before the high priest, and Peter denies him three times. || The Last Supper gives Christians the Eucharist or Lord’s Supper. Jesus prays “Abba, Father”. His answer at the trial, “I am”, quotes Daniel 7:13 and Psalm 110:1.
15 | Pilate hands Jesus over to be crucified. He is mocked, nailed to a cross, cries out “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” and dies. The temple curtain tears, and a centurion confesses him as the Son of God. || Jesus quotes Psalm 22:1 in Aramaic. The torn curtain and the Gentile soldier’s confession are Mark’s climax: the one mocked as king is revealed as God’s Son at the cross.
16 | On Sunday morning women find the tomb empty and a young man tells them Jesus has risen and will meet his disciples in Galilee. They flee in fear. Later verses tell of appearances and the great commission. || The oldest manuscripts end at 16:8, with the women afraid. Verses 9–20 were probably added early in the 100s; scholars debate whether Mark meant to end so abruptly or the ending was lost.
